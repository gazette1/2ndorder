# DGXX: Behind-the-meter power for AI data centers as grid capex flatlines

## Bear case first
The 10-Q names Cerebras and warns that the company's ability to continue as a going concern depends on obtaining significant funding [16]. Colocation revenue fell from $9.6M to $6.5M over six months [13], and insider open-market selling totaled $-272.5K (Form 4). Cerebras is the only named customer, so a delayed build has no offsetting revenue. The filing does not quantify construction cost and discloses that insufficient financing could delay or abandon the AI colocation obligations [19]. Share count rose 207.1 percent in 12 months with a shelf registration on file (reality check).

## Business today
DGXX sells cryptocurrency mining, energy and electricity, colocation services, and Tier III AI data center capacity [0][1]. Revenue was $34.2M and cash was $78.5M as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL). Management says the company is transitioning from Bitcoin mining to AI infrastructure [5].

## Chain position
DGXX occupies the data center operator and developer node, the direct beneficiary of behind-the-meter generation. On-site gas and solar plus storage shorten time to power and bypass utility interconnection queues. Faster deployment increases the leased or used capacity that drives data center revenue.

## Customer graph
Cerebras Systems Inc. is the sole named customer (enterprise, named). Federal award dollars are $0 (USASpending). Two other SEC filers name DGXX (customer graph). Customer evidence is thin: one contract, one counterparty, no government revenue.

## Insider and management signal
Form 4 data show net open-market selling of $-272.5K across 2 buys and 6 sales by 2 insiders, which is weak conviction. Management discloses specific terms: a 10-year term, a fixed take-or-pay fee of $195 per kW per month, phase-specific monthly fees, and defined readiness dates [4][9]. The commercial specificity contrasts with the insider selling.

## Street view
Coverage is effectively absent: unknown analyst count, consensus n/a, mean target n/a (sell-side aggregators). For a $406M market cap name with this contract visibility, thin coverage is itself part of the setup. Any initiation would be a new event, not a revision.

## Non-linear case
Full delivery of the 40 MW Cerebras campus implies roughly $93.6M in annualized colocation revenue, several times the current base [4][7]. For that to compound, Phase 1 must reach ready-for-service by December 15, 2026 and Phase 2 by the end of fiscal Q1 2027 [9], then be replicated at new sites. The precondition is financing, since the filing warns that insufficient funding could curtail the AI colocation obligations [19]. A second named customer would re-rate the equity off contracted backlog.

## Evidence
- Four reporting segments, including AI data centers [0][1].
- AI project assets grew from $11.0M to $59.0M from December 31, 2025 to June 30, 2026 [2].
- Ten-year colocation and master services agreement with Cerebras dated May 4, 2026 [3][6].
- Fixed take-or-pay fee of $195 per kW per month, about $2.925M per month for Phase 1 and $4.875M per month for Phase 2, escalating 3.0 percent annually [4].
- Total deployment of approximately 40 MW at the Columbiana, Alabama campus [7].
- One-time construction payments from Cerebras for both phases [8].
- Phase 1 ready-for-service targeted December 15, 2026; Phase 2 by end of first fiscal quarter of 2027 [9].
- Q2 2026 colocation revenue of $3.4M versus $4.5M in Q2 2025 [10][11].
- Six-month 2026 colocation revenue of $6.5M versus $9.6M in the prior period [13][14].
- Going concern language tied to Tier III initiatives and obtaining significant funding [16].
- Disclosure that insufficient financing may delay, curtail, or abandon AI colocation obligations [19].

## Sizing and survivability
At $33.4M average daily dollar volume (reality check), building a position takes about 8 trading days. Net cash of $78.5M (reality check) supports roughly 12.3 quarters of runway, but build-out costs are not quantified. The 207.1 percent share count increase and shelf registration on file (reality check) are real dilution risks.

## What would change our mind
- A Form 4 with open-market insider buying of meaningful size would counter the current selling signal.
- A second named colocation customer in a 10-Q or 10-K filing would reduce single-customer concentration.
- A federal award to DGXX would open a new customer class beyond the enterprise graph (USASpending).
