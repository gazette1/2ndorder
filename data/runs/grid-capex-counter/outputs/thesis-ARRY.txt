# ARRY: Avoid, flat grid capex and behind-the-meter margin compression pressure a tracker supplier with no direct revenue path

## Bear case first
The strongest disconfirming read is that ARRY sells equipment, not integration services, so the margin compression theme is indirect. The company states its cost advantage continues to drive adoption across distributed generation and utility-scale markets (filing [2]). No insider has sold stock in the trailing 12 months (Form 4), and the company holds $2.236M in federal awards (USASpending). The expected Q3 2026 closing of a transaction (filing [0]) could add capability. The short thesis requires EPC flow-down clauses (filing [3]) to pass margin cuts to subcontractors, which no filing yet shows, and the filings do not reveal a new business segment or named customers.

## Business today
ARRY sells solar tracking technology and fixed-tilt systems to utility-scale and distributed generation customers who construct, develop, and operate solar PV sites (filing [1][4]). Revenue was $1,284M and cash was $244.4M as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
The map position is behind-the-meter integrator margins, order 3, at risk, long horizon. ARRY sits at the equipment supply node upstream of those integrators. As a subcontractor to EPCs, ARRY faces flow-down clauses that tie its obligations to the EPC's end customer terms (filing [3]), making it a pass-through target for pricing pressure.

## Customer graph

Government: federal awards total $2.236M, all from the Department of Defense: $697.5K, $485.8K, and $416.3K (USASpending). The remaining $1,284M of revenue is unproven, so customer evidence is thin.

## Insider and management signal
Open-market insider activity is zero: 0 buys, 0 sales, 0 insiders (Form 4), which is absent conviction in either direction. Management language is generic, with no named programs or quantified targets tied to the theme (filing [1][3][4]).

## Street view
Sell-side coverage is unknown, with consensus and mean target not available. For a $704M market cap company with no visible analyst ratings, thin coverage is part of the setup: the short thesis is not crowded.

## Non-linear case
The downside compounds if easier financing draws new integrators into behind-the-meter solar and storage for data centers, and they bid away project margins. EPC flow-down clauses (filing [3]) transmit margin compression to ARRY. Utility-scale tracker pricing falls as grid capex flatlines near $180B annually and data center load is met by on-site gas turbines and behind-the-meter solar that never reaches ARRY's utility-scale channel. The $704M market cap would be a multiple lower if revenue stagnates and gross margin compresses. No filing evidence confirms this path, but none refutes it.

## Evidence
- Transaction expected to close in Q3 2026; ARRY describes itself as a global provider of solar tracking technology and fixed-tilt systems to utility-scale and distributed generation customers (filing [0]).
- Business overview repeats the tracker and fixed-tilt provider description (filing [1][4]).
- Cost advantage continues to drive widespread adoption across distributed generation and utility-scale markets (filing [2]).
- EPC flow-down clauses may make subcontractor obligations identical to the EPC's obligations to its end customer (filing [3]).

## Sizing and survivability

## What would change our mind
- A Form 4 with open-market insider buying would contradict the zero-conviction insider signal.
- An 8-K disclosing a named behind-the-meter or data center customer contract would show a revenue path tied to the theme.
- A 10-Q or 10-K showing stable or expanding gross margin would refute the margin compression mechanism.
