# SLNH: Behind-the-meter solar and storage integrator positioned for data center on-site generation demand

## Bear case first
A disconfirming read starts with the same evidence: one insider sold a net $649.2K over trailing 12 months (Form 4), no named customers appear in the excerpts, and federal awards total only $14.5K (USASpending). The balance sheet shows net cash of -$17.4M and cash runway of about 0.2 quarters (reality check). The behind-the-meter quotes describe the current business, not a new optionality, and the filings do not quantify revenue, market size, or contract pipeline. This is a survival story with an unproven demand thesis.

## Business today
The company builds behind the meter at renewable generation sites, accessing both on-site generation and existing grid interconnection infrastructure [0]. It also builds behind the meter to bypass long interconnection queues and source electricity directly from the generation site [1]. Revenue is $29.7M and cash is $462K (SEC XBRL), but the filing excerpts do not name the buyers.

## Chain position
The company occupies the behind-the-meter solar and storage integrator node. Its stated advantage is avoiding interconnection delays [1] while using existing grid infrastructure to improve power economics [0]. The adoption logic is that data center load growth is met on-site rather than through utility transmission and distribution capex, increasing project volume for integrators.

## Customer graph

## Street view

## Non-linear case
A multiple of today's business requires data center customers to commit to on-site solar and storage projects at scale. That would show up as named contracts in filings and would require flat utility T&D capex to keep grid connection unattractive. With cash runway of about 0.2 quarters (reality check), the company must also fund operations without further dilution. The current evidence does not show that path; it shows a liquidity constraint.

## Evidence

- Building behind the meter bypasses long interconnection queues and sources electricity directly from the generation site [1].
