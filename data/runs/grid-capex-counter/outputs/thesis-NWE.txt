# NWE: Flat grid capex stalls rate base growth; avoid

## Bear case first
Management guidance contradicts the short directly: 4-6% rate base and EPS growth from a 2024 base of $3.40 EPS and $5.38B rate base [0][1]. The $3.2B capital plan is funded by operations and debt, with equity delayed to 2027 [1][2]. The Black Hills merger adds scale and stability [4]; filings do not mention data center load behind the meter. Dossier signals are mixed: zero insider buying or selling (Form 4), federal customers at $209.2K (USASpending). The short thesis relies on an external grid-capex trend absent from company disclosures.

## Business today
NorthWestern is a fully regulated electric and gas utility; rate base is the primary earnings driver [8]. Coal generation contributes 7-8% of earnings [9]. Revenue was $414.3M and cash $8.8M as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
Node: rate-regulated electric utilities (order 1, at-risk, mid horizon). Under cost-of-service regulation, US grid capex flatlining near $180B annually stalls net rate base growth, the main driver of allowed revenue. Management targets 4-6% rate base growth [1], the central tension with the thesis.

## Customer graph
Government: $209.2K in federal awards from Health and Human Services (USASpending). Enterprise: no named customers in the excerpts. Unproven: the data center load that anchors the thesis. Two other filers name the company. Customer evidence is thin: regulated utility margin, not contracted enterprise demand, drives revenue.

## Insider and management signal
Insider conviction is neutral: no open-market buys or sells in 12 months (Form 4). Management is specific and committed: $3.68-$3.83 2026 EPS, 4-6% growth, $3.2B capex plan, 14% FFO/debt, named merger partner [1][2][3][4].

## Street view
Sell-side coverage is unknown: no analyst count, consensus, or mean target available. Thin coverage means the flat-capex scenario is likely unpriced. That coverage gap is part of the setup.

## Non-linear case
If the thesis is correct, utility T&D capex growth stays below 1%, and NorthWestern's 4-6% rate base growth guidance breaks. Equity issuances in 2027 dilute shareholders into a stagnant rate base [2]. The Black Hills merger adds integration risk, and coal generation at 7-8% of earnings [9] becomes a stranded-asset liability as environmental costs rise. The gas revenue requirement steps down with field depletion [6]. The compounding path is a de-rating from growth utility to bond-like multiple.

## Evidence
- 2026 EPS guidance of $3.68-$3.83 affirmed; 4-6% EPS and rate base growth from 2024 base [0][1].
- $3.2B 5-year capital plan, about $300M for South Dakota generation, funded by operations and debt [1].
- Equity issuances expected beginning 2027; FFO/debt target of 14% [2].
- Black Hills merger described as a strategic combination creating scale and stability [3][4].
- NorthWestern is fully regulated; rate base drives earnings [8].
- Coal generation provides about 7-8% of earnings [9].
- Montana gas revenue requirement steps down with natural gas production depletion [6].

## Sizing and survivability
Market cap $4.3B with $33.5M average daily dollar volume, about 8 trading days to build a position (delayed price data). Net cash is -$3.28B against $8.8M cash (SEC XBRL), and equity issuances begin in 2027 [2]; share count is up 0.2% in 12 months (SEC XBRL). The debt load and equity pipeline threaten the thesis if rate base growth stalls.

## What would change our mind
- A Form 4 showing insider open-market buying.
- A 10-K or 8-K filing linking data center load to new T&D investment.
- A federal award or customer contract showing contracted enterprise revenue.
