# SLNH: Behind-the-meter solar and storage integrator should benefit if data center on-site generation grows while utility T&D capex stays flat.

## Bear case first

The bear case is that Soluna is a micro-cap cash consumer with no visible customer pipeline. The 10-K language describes a development approach, not a contracted backlog, and the only federal awards are $14.45M combined (USASpending). Insider open-market sales netted -$649.219M over 12 months (Form 4), and the share count rose 711.4 percent in the same period (SEC XBRL), pointing to dilution rather than value creation. Revenue is $29.717MM, but net cash is -$17.437MM and cash runway is about 0.2 quarters (SEC XBRL). Flat utility capex could also mean fewer transmission opportunities and less demand for any grid-adjacent developer.

## Business today

Soluna builds behind-the-meter generation at renewable sites, using on-site generation and existing grid interconnection infrastructure to improve power economics and accelerate timelines [0]. It bypasses long interconnection queues and sources electricity directly from the generation site [1]. Revenue was $29.717MM and cash was $462M as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position

In the seed thesis, Soluna occupies the behind-the-meter solar and storage integrator node. Each data center project bundles modules, inverters, and batteries, so integrator revenue rises with project count. Behind-the-meter projects avoid transmission costs and interconnection queues, making them faster to deploy than grid-connected renewables.

## Customer graph

Customer evidence is thin. Enterprise buyers: none named. Government buyers: federal awards total $14.45M, with the Department of Veterans Affairs at $10.69M and the Department of Defense at $3.76M (USASpending). One other filer names Soluna, which counts as an unproven buyer.

## Insider and management signal

Trailing 12-month Form 4 open-market activity is net -$649.219M, from 3 buys and 30 sales by one insider (Form 4). That is selling. Management language cites better power economics, faster timelines, and queue avoidance [0][1], but the excerpts contain no named programs, capital commitments, or quantified targets.

## Street view

Consensus is n/a, the mean target is n/a, and no analyst firm is listed in the aggregator data. Reports are paywalled and not linked. For a micro-cap with thin sell-side attention, the lack of coverage is part of the setup.

## Non-linear case

The non-linear case relies on the seed thesis: data center load growth is met by on-site gas turbines and solar plus storage behind the meter, keeping utility T&D capex flat. Soluna would be an order-1 beneficiary as project volume rises. Each behind-the-meter project bundles modules, inverters, and batteries, so revenue scales with installed units. To deliver a multiple, Soluna must convert its development approach into named customer contracts and positive cash flow. The filing excerpts do not currently show that pipeline.

## Evidence

- By building behind the meter at renewable generation sites, Soluna accesses on-site generation and existing grid interconnection infrastructure, which can improve power economics and accelerate development timelines [0].
- Behind-the-meter development bypasses long interconnection queues and sources electricity directly from the generation site [1].

- A Form 4 open-market buy by an insider after 12 months of net selling.
