# DGXX: Behind-the-meter generation turns a 40 MW Cerebras contract into an AI revenue base above current revenue

## Bear case first
Insider conviction is the weak subscore: two insiders sold a net $-272M in the trailing 12 months (Form 4). The customer graph is thin, with one named enterprise customer and zero federal awards (USASpending). Colocation revenue fell in Q2 2026 versus Q2 2025 during the AI transition [10][11]. The filing warns of going concern and financing risk [16][19]. Missed phase dates [9] would delay or eliminate the contracted revenue.

## Business today
Four operating segments: cryptocurrency mining, energy sales, colocation services, and Tier III AI data centers [0][1]. Total revenue was $34.19MM and cash was $78.48MM as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL). Colocation revenue was $6.45MM for the six months ended June 30, 2026 [13].

## Chain position
Node: data center operator and developer, first-order beneficiary, near-term horizon. Behind-the-meter generation bypasses utility interconnection queues, shortening time to power and letting data centers monetize capacity sooner. The Tier III AI data centers are the monetization asset [1].

## Customer graph
Enterprise: Cerebras Systems Inc., the sole named customer, under a 10-year agreement [3]. Government: $0 in federal awards (USASpending). Two other filers name the company (SEC XBRL). The filing does not attribute the reported colocation revenue to a named payer [13], so customer evidence is thin.

## Insider and management signal
Net open-market selling: $-272M across 2 buys and 6 sales by 2 insiders (Form 4). Management discloses specific terms: $195 per kW per month, 3% annual escalation, one-time construction payments, defined phase dates [4][8][9]. The filing states a transition from Bitcoin mining to AI infrastructure [5].

## Street view
Analyst count, consensus, and mean target are n/a, and no recent rating actions are available (Street view). Thin coverage is part of the setup.

## Non-linear case
If Phase 1 (15 MW) and Phase 2 (40 MW) hit their targets [9], the Cerebras contract implies roughly $93.6MM in annualized fees [4], against $34.19MM total revenue (SEC XBRL). AI segment assets grew from $11.02MM to $58.99MM in six months [2]. One-time construction payments [8] are unquantified, so the funding gap is an open question. The multiple outcome requires on-time construction, financing, and customers beyond Cerebras.
