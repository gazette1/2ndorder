# FVR: Avoid as a high-conviction short; the at-risk node is absent, and leverage plus dilution are the only live risks

## Bear case first

The name fails as a high-conviction short. The fixed charge and covenant disclosures [0][1][2][3] show a REIT with near-term protection: 3.6x fixed charge coverage [0] and a 1.50 to 1.00 fixed charge covenant [3]. No filing mentions standby charges, behind-the-meter generation, or any energy segment. Insider open-market trades are $0 (Form 4), and customer evidence is empty, but those gaps cut against a thematic short. A short would have to rely on credit stress or dilution, not the mapped tariff mechanism.

## Business today

The 10-K excerpts do not describe the revenue model, product, or tenants; they report covenants and contractual obligations [3]. Revenue was $67.115MM and cash was $13.518MM at 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL). The Adjusted EBITDAre and fixed charge language [0] identifies a REIT, but no buyer is named.

## Chain position

The map assigns FVR to the behind-the-meter generation economics node under standby charges (order 2, at-risk, horizon mid). Approved standby tariffs would reduce the savings that justify on-site generation. No filing evidence places FVR in that chain, so the chain position is label only.

## Non-linear case

The downside path is covenant breach. If adjusted EBITDA falls or fixed charges rise, the 1.50 to 1.00 fixed charge covenant [3] is the tripwire. If regulators approve standby charges, on-site generation economics deteriorate; that would matter only if FVR's tenants are data centers or energy users, which the filings do not show. The more direct compounding scenario is balance sheet stress: negative net cash of -$300.733MM, shares up 28.1%, and a shelf registration (reality check) imply future equity issuance. A forced capital raise could turn a modest decline into a multiple of downside.
