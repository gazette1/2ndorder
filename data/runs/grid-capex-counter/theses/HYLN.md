# HYLN: A distributed generator for behind-the-meter data centers, not the utility standby revenue collector

## Bear case first
The grid capex flatline thesis benefits utility standby revenue, not Hyliion. Filings describe a generator with no named customer and no dated order milestone [0]-[9]. Cash was $22.938MM and runway is about two quarters (Reality check), so dilution is the base case. Federal awards fund development, not repeatable revenue (USASpending).

## Business today
Hyliion develops the KARNO Power Module, a distributed generator that differs from reciprocating engines and gas turbines [0] and targets 200 kW to low megawatts [3]. It is positioned for backup power, grid balancing, and microgrids with storage and renewables [4][5][6][7]. Revenue was $3.475MM and cash was $22.938MM as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
Hyliion is an equipment supplier upstream of the order 3 node in the thesis, which is utility standby revenue. In the thesis, data centers with on-site generation retain a grid connection, creating standby fees, but Hyliion would earn from equipment sales, not fees. Locating the module near consumption avoids 5-10% transmission and distribution losses [1], supporting the behind-the-meter case.

## Non-linear case
The non-linear path is from development awards to commercial orders. If KARNO becomes a standard backup or grid-balancing generator for data center microgrids, one program could require many modules and produce revenue far above the $3.475MM base (SEC XBRL). That requires cost, reliability, and emissions performance, plus named customers in the backup power [6] and grid balancing markets [7]. Government awards show technical credibility, not commercial scale.

## Evidence
- The KARNO Power Module is distinguished from systems that rely on reciprocating engines or gas turbines to drive a rotating shaft [0].
- Combined cycle gas turbine plants can exceed 50% efficiency but incur 5-10% T&D losses, which KARNO is expected to avoid by locating near consumption [1].
- Hyliion says KARNO is a distributed generation solution [2].
- Initial target applications are 200 kW to the low megawatt range [3].
- KARNO can integrate with energy storage and renewables [4].
- With storage and solar or wind, KARNO can enable microgrids for EV charging [5].
- The backup power market is well established and positioned to grow [6].
- The grid balancing and servicing market is expanding [7].
- Resiliency-as-a-Service and Virtual Power Plants are emerging business models [8].
- KARNO can offer a lower-emissions option for backup power if regulations tighten [9].
