# ARRY: Tracker supplier with no offset to behind-the-meter margin compression

## Bear case first
The short fails if tracker hardware pricing holds while integrator margins compress. ARRY sells solar tracking technology to utility-scale and distributed generation customers [0][1], and the at-risk node is downstream integrator margin, not equipment pricing. The dossier shows no insider selling (Form 4), removing a confirmation signal for the short. Customer evidence is thin, with only $2.2MM in federal awards (USASpending), which weakens a bearish concentration argument but also leaves the revenue base unvalidated. The closest filing link to the theme is the EPC flow-down clause risk in [3], which is a contractual risk, not proven margin erosion.

## Business today
ARRY is a global provider of solar tracking technology and fixed-tilt systems to utility-scale and distributed generation customers who construct, develop, and operate solar PV sites [0][1][4]. Revenue was $1,284MM and cash was $244MM as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL). The company says its cost advantage drives adoption across distributed generation and utility-scale markets [2].

## Chain position
ARRY sits at the equipment supply node upstream of the at-risk behind-the-meter integrator node. The theme's mechanism is integrator margin compression as capital floods into behind-the-meter solar and storage; ARRY gets paid for trackers regardless of the integrator's profit per project, so the pass-through is indirect. The node is order 3 with a long horizon, meaning the pressure compounds slowly and may take years to reach tracker pricing.

## Non-linear case
The short compounds if integrator margin compression spreads upstream to hardware pricing. If data center load is met by on-site gas turbines rather than solar-plus-storage, tracker demand stalls and the $1,284MM revenue base contracts. Flow-down clauses in [3] become the mechanism: EPCs pass concessions to subcontractors, and ARRY accepts thinner margins to hold volume. The negative net cash position of $-184MM (SEC XBRL) turns a revenue decline into a liquidity event, and the 1% annual share count creep accelerates if equity is issued.

## What would change our mind
- Form 4 open-market insider purchases by officers or directors, which would contradict the avoid stance.
- A 10-Q or 10-K reporting stable or expanding gross margin in the tracker business, which would show the flow-down risk in [3] is not repricing hardware.
- A named customer award in an 8-K or federal award database tying ARRY to a behind-the-meter data center project, which would make the integrator margin theme a demand story rather than a margin risk.
