# NGS: Gas turbine aftermarket thesis is unproven; current aftermarket services are compressor call-out and commissioning work, low margin and insignificant

## Bear case first
Filings describe aftermarket services as call-out services on customer-owned equipment and commissioning of new units, not gas turbine maintenance [0][4]. Aftermarket services represented an insignificant portion of overall gross margins in 2025 and 2024 [6]. The data center thesis requires an installed base of aeroderivative turbines and long-term OEM service contracts, none of which appears in the evidence. Insider open-market selling was net $7.8MM over 12 months (Form 4), and no named customers or federal awards appear in the customer graph. The strongest disconfirming read is that NGS is a compressor rental and sales business with a small, lower-margin service adjunct, not a gas turbine aftermarket beneficiary.

## Business today
NGS reports three operating units: Rental, Sales, and Aftermarket Services [1]. Rental agreements include a fee for servicing the compressor unit, and Aftermarket Services provides routine or call-out services on customer-owned equipment plus commissioning of new units [3][4]. Dossier fundamentals report revenue of $15.0MM and cash of $0 as of 2023-12-31 (SEC XBRL). The filings do not name end customers.

## Chain position
The map assigns NGS to the beneficiary node in the gas turbine aftermarket chain, where new turbine installations produce 20 years of parts and overhaul revenue. The actual business occupies the compressor services node: call-out and commissioning work around customer-owned compression equipment [4]. No filing connects that service capability to gas turbines, data centers, or OEM maintenance contracts.

## Customer graph
No enterprise customer is named in the excerpts (Customer graph). Federal awards are $0 (USASpending). One other filer names the company (Customer graph). The paying base for aftermarket services is therefore unproven, and customer concentration cannot be assessed from the available evidence.

## Insider and management signal
Form 4 trailing 12 months shows net open-market selling of $7.8MM across 3 buys and 23 sales by one insider (Form 4). Management lists Aftermarket Services as an operating unit [1] but provides no named programs, committed capex, or quantified targets [4]. The language describes routine call-out work, not a strategic gas turbine service expansion.

## Street view
No sell-side coverage is identified: analyst count unknown, consensus n/a, mean target n/a. No firm or rating is available. Thin coverage is consistent with a small-cap that has not been broadly evaluated.

## Non-linear case
The non-linear case would require NGS to convert call-out service capability into multi-year gas turbine maintenance contracts for data center or on-site power customers. It would have to disclose named customers, contract wins, or service revenue that is material to gross margins. None of that appears in the filings. The path to a multiple therefore rests on future disclosures, not current evidence.

## Sizing and survivability
Average daily dollar volume is $5.4MM, which is about 46 trading days to build a position (Reality check). Net cash is $0 (SEC XBRL) and share count rose 2.9 percent in 12 months (Reality check). A small fund can trade the name, but zero cash and share dilution threaten a long-dated aftermarket thesis unless operating cash flow is stronger than the dossier shows.

## What would change our mind

2. A Form 4 showing open-market insider buying, reversing the trailing 12-month net selling.
