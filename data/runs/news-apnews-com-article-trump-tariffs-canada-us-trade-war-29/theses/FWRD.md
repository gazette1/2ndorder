# FWRD: US-Canada tariff escalation reduces cross-border freight volumes and pressures per-shipment revenue

## Bear case first
The short fails because the tariff thesis is unquantified. Trade policy is one generic volume risk among many in the 10-K [8][9][10][18]. Customs brokerage and compliance services [2][4][6] could see more work per shipment as tariffs add documentation, and Form 4 shows zero insider selling (Form 4). The customer roster is unproven but not concentrated, and no named customer cancellation appears in the filings.

## Business today
Forward Air sells time-sensitive freight services. Expedited Freight provides truckload, consolidation, warehousing and customs brokerage [0][2]; Omni provides freight forwarding, fulfillment, customs brokerage and distribution [1][3][5]. Customers are US-based businesses operating domestically and internationally [1][3]. Revenue was $2,495.1M and cash was $106.0M (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
Forward Air sits at the freight and customs brokerage node on US-Canada lanes, marked at-risk with a near horizon. Tariffs reduce the quantity demanded of imported goods; the firm earns per shipment, so revenue falls with shipment counts. The lane is high-frequency and just-in-time, with no buffer inventory.

## Non-linear case
The downside compounds if tariffs escalate through 2025-2026 and importers cut or delay replenishment. Volume falls first, then revenue, while fixed costs hold. Net cash is -$1,581.3M (Reality check), so a revenue decline pressures debt service and covenants. Share count is up 11% in 12 months (Reality check), and any shortfall is likely funded with dilution.
