# TR: Tariff-driven input cost pressure with limited pass-through and no growth optionality

## Bear case first
The short case fails if TR's price pass-through history repeats. TR has raised prices to recover input costs [2] and implemented price increases to mitigate input cost increases [15]. It can also change product sizes in response to input costs [3]. The filings do not quantify Canada-sourced input exposure or show customer concentration, and there is no open-market insider selling (Form 4). The customer graph is too thin to support a demand-collapse short.

## Business today
TR sells consumer products to customers and final consumers [0]. It ships product between U.S. and Canada or Mexico manufacturing operations [9]. Revenue was $732.524M and cash was $127.165M at 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
TR is the downstream customer node. Domestic input producers gain tariff-umbrella pricing power and pass inflation to TR; TR cannot switch to imports because tariffs make imports equally expensive. TR's filing confirms tariffs on imports from Canada could materially affect input costs [11].
