# TE: Tariff cost pressure on a rigid solar base, with no customer proof and active dilution

## Bear case first
Tariffs appear only as a risk to G2_Austin construction costs and equipment lead times [5], not as a demand driver. Customer evidence is empty: zero federal awards, no named buyers, no other filer identifies TE (Customer graph). Open-market insider selling totals $1.87M with zero buys over 12 months (Form 4). The disconfirming reading is that transferable 45X tax credits under Section 6418 can subsidize U.S. production through August 2026 [2], and the $755.3M revenue base funds the buildout. Filings do not quantify the credit, the tariff hit, or any offtake, so the protection is structural, not proven.

## Business today
T1 Energy, f/k/a Trina Solar US Manufacturing Holding Inc., acquired G1 and related U.S. solar entities [0] and manufactures PV modules and cells at G1_Dallas under a non-exclusive IP license from TUS [3]. Revenue is $755.3M and cash is $182.45M (SEC XBRL). TUS earns 5% of combined adjusted EBITDA of G1 or G1 plus G2_Austin [1][4].

## Chain position
TE sits at the rigid cross-border production node: solar manufacturing that cannot relocate quickly if tariffs raise input costs. The filings cite tariffs and long lead times as cost risks [5] but do not name Canada or cross-border production. The margin step-down is a near-term input cost, not a demand signal.

## Customer graph
Unproven. Federal awards are $0 (USASpending), there are 0 named customers, and 0 other filers name the company. The IP license allows U.S. sales [3], but the filings do not disclose who buys the output. Government and enterprise buyers are unproven from the evidence.

## Insider and management signal
Open-market activity over 12 months is net negative at $1.87M sold across 4 sales and 0 buys (Form 4). Management names G1 and G2_Austin, ties TUS fees to adjusted EBITDA [4], and cites the 45X credit [2], but gives no capex target, output goal, or tariff mitigation plan. The language shows operational commitment, not financial conviction.

## Street view
Sell-side coverage is unproven: analyst count, consensus, and mean target are all n/a. Thin coverage is consistent with the 8/100 composite score and means the short has no published counterweight.

## Non-linear case
The downside compounds if tariff-driven construction costs at G2_Austin exceed plan and the 45X credit, the only stated offset, steps down after August 2026 [2]. With zero named customers and no federal awards, TE is forced to sell modules at spot prices into the inflated cost base. The shelf and the 88.9% share-count increase fund the shortfall, so the bear case moves from margin step-down to solvency.

## Evidence
- T1 Energy is the Delaware holding company, f/k/a Trina Solar US Manufacturing Holding Inc., that acquired G1, G1 Midco, G1 Associated, and TUM2 [0].
- TUS receives an annual fee equal to 5% of combined adjusted EBITDA of G1 or G1 and G2_Austin, as applicable [1][4].
- The 45X tax credit is referenced with a Section 6418 transfer election and an August 15, 2026 date [2].
- The IP license grants a non-exclusive right to manufacture and sell PV modules and cells at G1_Dallas or other approved facilities [3].
- Tariffs, trade policy, and long lead times are disclosed as risks affecting G2_Austin construction costs and equipment availability [5].

## Sizing and survivability
At $277.1M average daily dollar volume, a small fund can build a position in about 1 day (Reality check). Net cash is negative at $207.7M, cash is $182.45M, and shares are up 88.9% with a shelf on file (Reality check). Dilution is part of the short thesis, not a barrier.

## What would change our mind
- An open-market insider buy on a Form 4 would contradict the selling signal.
- A named customer or federal award in a filing or USASpending would prove demand.
- A 10-Q or 10-K quantifying 45X credit receipts or tariff passthrough would reset the margin math.
