# NNBR: Automation component maker whose own filings call tariffs a cost and demand risk, not a reshoring opportunity

## Bear case first
The 10-K describes tariffs as a threat to input costs and customer demand, not a source of new business [2][3][4]. The excerpts contain no design wins, qualification milestones, capacity additions, or named customer programs tied to reshoring. Form 4 shows net open-market selling of $45.3K over the trailing 12 months, and the customer graph shows no named enterprise buyers and zero federal awards (Form 4, USASpending). The most direct reading is that NN is a tariff loser, not a beneficiary.

## Business today
NN designs and manufactures high-precision components and assemblies for a variety of end markets on a global basis [0]. Revenue was $422.2M and cash was $11.4M as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
NN sits at the automation component supplier node, order 3 beneficiary, mid horizon. The thesis logic is that automation equipment orders translate into component orders one to two quarters later. The filings do not describe NN's backlog or order flow in those terms; NN identifies only as a component manufacturer, so the chain position is inferred, not disclosed.

## Customer graph
No named enterprise customers appear in the excerpts. Federal award dollars are $0 (USASpending). Three other filers name NN, which is thin corroboration. Customer validation is unproven from the evidence available.

## Insider and management signal
Open-market insider activity was net selling of $45.3K, with 0 buys and 1 sale in the trailing 12 months (Form 4). Management language is generic risk-factor boilerplate: the company says it continues to monitor tariffs and macroeconomic conditions [7]. No committed capex or quantified reshoring targets appear.

## Street view
No sell-side analyst coverage, consensus, or mean target is available from the aggregators consulted. For a $298M market cap name with no coverage, the thin Street view is itself a setup, but it also means no external validation of the thesis.

## Non-linear case
For NN to become a multiple of today's business, tariff-driven reshoring would have to convert into named automation customer programs and visible backlog growth. That path would require NN to appear as a supplier in customer filings, to disclose capacity additions in a 10-K or 10-Q, and to show insider buying or a federal award. None of those signals are present in the current evidence.

## Evidence
- NN is a diversified industrial company that designs and manufactures high-precision components and assemblies for a variety of end markets globally [0].
- Geopolitical conflicts, tariffs, and inflation have made metal prices more volatile [1].
- Import tariffs and trade regulations could significantly increase prices NN pays for raw materials [2].
- Tariffs could reduce customer demand for the components NN manufactures [3].
- Reduced customer demand from tariffs could have a material adverse effect on NN's business and liquidity [4].
- Supply disruptions, especially in China, and inflationary cost pressures have hurt manufacturing and demand [5].
- Proposals could result in increased customs duties and renegotiation of U.S. trade agreements [6].
- NN monitors impacts of global trade negotiations, tariffs, inflationary cost pressures, and supply chain disruptions [7].
- Tariffs may increase inflation generally and make precious metals harder to procure [8].

## Sizing and survivability
Average daily dollar volume is $13.0M, so a small fund needs about 19 days to build a position (reality check). Net cash is negative at $-145.7M, cash is $11.4M, and share count is up 64.2% in 12 months with a shelf on file (reality check). These dilution and balance sheet facts threaten the thesis on their own.

## What would change our mind
A 10-Q or 10-K disclosing named automation customers, backlog growth, or capacity additions tied to reshoring. A Form 4 showing open-market insider buying rather than selling. A USASpending federal award naming NN as a supplier.
