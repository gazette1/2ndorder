# ONEW: Floor-plan borrower exposed to tariff-driven credit contraction

## Bear case first
Why this name fails: a short needs a near-term trigger, and the filings do not provide one. ONEW was in compliance with all covenants as of June 30, 2026 [15], floor-plan rates on new inventory declined from 7.17%-9.42% at September 30, 2025 to 6.50%-8.75% at June 30, 2026 [3][4], and the only insider activity is net buying, not selling (Form 4). The filings describe tariffs only as a risk that could reduce consumer spending [10] and say the company cannot predict their effect [11]. The disconfirming read is that the dossier shows no insider sales and no named customer concentration, so the short lacks a trigger.

## Business today
OneWater Marine buys boats and marine products from certain manufacturers and finances those purchases through a wholesale inventory financing program [0][1][16][17]. Cash needs for inventory have historically been financed with the Inventory Financing Facility [13]. Revenue was $1,872,334,000 and cash was $64,820,000 as of September 30, 2025 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
ONEW is the borrower in the capital-provider-to-cross-border-importer node. It relies on a collateralized inventory financing facility [1][7][14], and the economic logic is that a lender marks down inventory collateral and cuts advance rates when tariffs erode liquidation value. The facility covenants tie capital availability to leverage, fixed charges, and liquidity [6], which is exactly where a credit contraction would hit.

## Insider and management signal
One insider bought $1,098,619 net across six open-market purchases and zero sales in the trailing 12 months (Form 4). Management language is speculative: tariffs could reduce consumer spending and the company cannot predict their effect [10][11]. No named tariff-related investment, capital expenditure, or target appears in the excerpts.

## Street view
Sell-side aggregators show no analyst count, no consensus, and no mean target (street view). No firm rating or price target is available. Thin coverage means the stock is not a crowded short and any negative news could be repriced slowly.

## Non-linear case
The downside compounds if tariff escalation raises floor-plan borrowing costs relative to collateral values. ONEW's borrowing cost is SOFR plus 2.75% to 5.00% depending on inventory age [18], and the facility is secured by inventory and related assets [7]. If lenders cut advance rates due to lower liquidation values, ONEW must either use cash to fund inventory or sell at discounts. A breach of the funded debt/EBITDA or fixed-charge covenants [6] could accelerate the facility, and distribution restrictions in the credit facilities [12] would limit shareholder recovery. That path is a credit-access spiral, not a demand-only decline.

## Sizing and survivability
Average daily dollar volume is $1,243,294, implying about 198 trading days to build a position (reality check). Net cash is negative $269,377,000 (reality check), so the balance sheet relies on the floor-plan facility and lender support. A small fund can own this only as an illiquid short, and the lack of a near-term catalyst plus no dilution data makes the position difficult to size.

## What would change our mind
A Form 4 insider sale would break the current buy-only signal. A 10-Q or 8-K disclosing a covenant waiver, borrowing-base reduction, or collateral call would confirm the credit-tightening mechanism. A 10-Q quantifying Canadian-sourced inventory or tariff cost would give the short a measurable trigger.
