# EAF: Tariff-hit intermediate goods importer with no pricing power evidence and net debt (short/avoid)

## Bear case first
The short fails if management's cost guidance holds: cash COGS per MT is expected to decline low single digits in 2026 [1], and Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA fell only $1M despite lower realized prices [0]. The filings do not quantify tariff exposure, import share, or contract pass-through terms, so the at-risk node may not apply to EAF's actual input mix. No insider selling has occurred (Form 4), which removes one short confirmation signal.

## Business today
Revenue was $504.134M and cash $176.878M (SEC XBRL). The cited excerpts do not name the product line; they describe a manufacturer selling output by metric ton, with by-product deliveries included in COGS [4][7]. Adjusted EBITDA was $2M in Q2 2026 versus $3M in Q2 2025, on lower realized prices partially offset by lower cash COGS per MT [0].

## Chain position
EAF occupies the order-1 node: a cross-border importer of tariffed intermediate goods. A tariff is a direct variable cost on each imported unit, so gross margin compresses before volumes adjust if contracts fix prices. The cited filings do not disclose tariff rate or import share of COGS.

## Customer graph

No named enterprise customers appear in the excerpts; four other filers name EAF (USASpending). Relative to $504.134M revenue, government awards are immaterial, and customer evidence is thin.

## Insider and management signal
No insider bought or sold in the trailing 12 months: $0 net across 0 transactions (Form 4). Management language is generic, covering volume, pricing, revenue, capex, and COGS with no tariff or regionalization commentary [2]. This is neutral, not a conviction signal.

## Street view

## Non-linear case
The short compounds if tariff costs hit COGS while realized prices keep falling. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA was already only $2M [0], so a gross margin miss turns that number negative. Net cash is negative $917.828M (reality check), meaning net debt of about $917.8M; an 8.7-quarter cash runway shortens once EBITDA turns negative. The shelf registration and the 89.9% share count decline over 12 months (reality check) leave no buffer for another adverse year.

## Evidence
- Adjusted EBITDA was $2M in Q2 2026 versus $3M in Q2 2025, reflecting lower weighted-average realized price partly offset by lower cash COGS per MT [0].
- Management expects cash COGS per MT to decline low single digits in 2026 versus 2025 [1].
- Forward-looking statements cover volume, pricing, revenue, capex, and COGS; none mention tariffs or regionalization [2].
- Cash COGS per MT is COGS less D&A and less COGS from by-product deliveries, divided by sales volume in MT [4].
- By-product delivery COGS is the COGS associated with sales of manufacturing by-products [7].

## Sizing and survivability
A position would take about 100 trading days to build at the $2.469635M average daily dollar volume (reality check), feasible for a small fund but slow. Cash on hand is $176.878M (SEC XBRL); net cash is negative $917.828M (reality check), implying about $1.095B of debt, and cash runway is about 8.7 quarters (reality check). The shelf registration creates dilution risk; for a short, low liquidity also makes covering expensive.

## What would change our mind
- A Form 4 showing a material open-market insider purchase would signal tariff costs are manageable.
- A 10-Q or 8-K disclosing import share of COGS or tariff surcharges in customer contracts would confirm or refute the pass-through failure.
- A federal award naming EAF in a supply chain regionalization program would add the customer validation that is absent today (USASpending).
