# GRAFTECH INTERNATIONAL LTD (EAF): Tariff relief lowers input costs; filings show no revenue catalyst

## Bear case first
The tariff theme is absent from the evidence. Management expects a low single-digit decline in cash cost of goods sold per MT in 2026, without attributing it to tariffs [1]. Adjusted EBITDA fell to $2M from $3M, so cost relief did not offset price decline [0]. Form 4 shows no insider activity; $2.0M federal awards are small against $504.1M revenue (Form 4, USASpending, SEC XBRL). No filing excerpt names a customer, segment, or dated catalyst.

## Business today
The filing evidence describes a manufacturer with sales volume in metric tons and by-product deliveries in sales [4][7]. Revenue was $504.1M and cash was $176.9M at 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL). The product and end customer are not identified.

## Chain position
The position is cross-border input cost relief, order 1 beneficiary, near horizon. Cash cost of goods sold per MT is tracked and expected down [1][4]. Tariff removal would reduce Canadian input costs, but Canadian content is not disclosed.

## Customer graph
Government buyer: Department of Energy awarded $2.0M, with awards of $1.9M, $466.7K, and -$348.1K (USASpending). No enterprise customer is named (USASpending). Four other filers name the company (USASpending). Customer evidence is thin.

## Insider and management signal
Form 4 shows zero insider open-market activity over 12 months (Form 4). That is neutral. Management gives a cost target but does not link it to tariffs [1].

## Street view
Sell-side aggregator data list no analyst count, consensus, or mean target (sell-side aggregators). No firm rating actions are reported in the same data. Thin coverage is part of the setup but offers no validation.

## Non-linear case
The non-linear path needs volume and pricing upside, not just cost relief. If Canadian input costs fall more than the low single-digit expectation [1], and integrated supply chains support demand, adjusted EBITDA could move well above $2M [0]. The evidence does not show that path. Canadian materials must be material inputs, and price competition must not pass the gain away.

## Evidence
- Adjusted EBITDA was $2M in Q2 2026 versus $3M in Q2 2025; lower realized price was partially offset by lower cash COGS per MT [0].
- 2026 cash COGS per MT is expected to decline a low single-digit percentage [1].
- Forward-looking statements include volume, pricing, revenue, capex, and COGS [2].
- Non-GAAP measures include EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted net loss, adjusted loss per share, free cash flow, adjusted free cash flow, net debt, and cash COGS per MT [3].
- Cash COGS per MT is COGS less depreciation and amortization and less by-product COGS, divided by sales volume in MT [4].
- Non-GAAP measures should be considered alongside GAAP measures [5].
- A note reflects depreciation and amortization in COGS [6].
- A note reflects COGS from by-product deliveries [7].

## Sizing and survivability
Average daily dollar volume is $2.5M, about 100 trading days to build a position (reality check). Net cash is -$917.8M and cash runway is about 8.7 quarters (reality check). Share count fell 89.9% in 12 months and a shelf registration is on file, so dilution risk threatens the thesis (reality check).

## What would change our mind
An 8-K or 10-Q that discloses Canadian raw material sourcing or a tariff cost line would make the mechanism observable. A Form 4 open-market insider purchase would add conviction. A USASpending award would show federal demand; a revised cash COGS guidance larger than low single digit [1] would show in an 8-K or 10-Q.
