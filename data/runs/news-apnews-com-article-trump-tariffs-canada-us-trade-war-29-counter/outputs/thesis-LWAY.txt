# LWAY: Import competition compresses margins after tariff resolution

## Bear case first
The disconfirming reading is that the 2027 margin recovery [0] is a hope, not a plan. The same filing lists price competition and customer decisions as risks [1], and the 10-K says price competition has already hurt results [3]. Tariff resolution brings import competition; the company would cut prices or lose share [2]. In a high-fixed-cost dairy business, price cuts hit gross margin first. Dossier evidence is thin: insider buying is $50.5K (Form 4), federal awards are $0 (USASpending), and no other filer names it (customer graph). This is a short or avoid.

## Business today
Sells products tied to milk costs [6] and customer acceptance [1]. Revenue was $212.496M and cash was $5.571M as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL). No customer names are disclosed in the cited excerpts.

## Chain position
The thesis maps it as an import-competing producer at an at-risk node, order 2, mid horizon. Tariff resolution forces a choice between lower prices and lost share [2]. Milk costs do not fall with selling prices [6], so gross margin compresses.

## Non-linear case
If tariff resolution brings Canadian product in, the company cuts price to hold share [2]. Sticky milk costs [6] compress gross margin, and fixed costs amplify losses. Cash is $5.571M and net cash is $2.821M (SEC XBRL, reality check), a thin buffer. Missing the 2027 recovery [0] could trigger a shelf draw or a forced sale.

## Sizing and survivability
About 50 trading days to build at $4.94M average daily dollar volume (reality check); exits could be slow. Cash is $5.571M and net cash is $2.821M (SEC XBRL, reality check). A shelf registration is on file and share count is down 0.8% (reality check); a cash raise would add supply and support the short.

## What would change our mind

If a federal award appears in USASpending or another filer names the company as a customer, the revenue base is more protected.
