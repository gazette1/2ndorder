# OLN: Canadian margin normalization is a long-horizon overhang, but government awards and limited Canadian exposure keep the short from being immediate

## Bear case first

The short fails because OLN's Canadian footprint is small. Canada is 2% of employees and one chlor-alkali plant [2][0]; no Canadian labor agreements expire in 2026 [3]. Direct tariff impact on chemicals has not been significant [10], and management is only monitoring [12]. DoD and RCMP awards provide a government buyer [1]. Filings show no dated catalyst or announced capacity tying Canadian supplier margin normalization to OLN. Insider selling and DoD concentration are cautionary, not proof.

## Business today

OLN sells chlor-alkali products and vinyls [0], epoxy [17], and Winchester ammunition [15]. End uses include pulp and paper processing, chemical manufacturing, water purification, vinyl chloride, and urethane chemicals [0]. Revenue was $6,780.8M and cash was $167.6M (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position

OLN's exposure is the Becancour chlor-alkali plant [0]; new capacity and utilization are external margin factors [4].

## Non-linear case

Downside compounds if retaliatory tariffs on caustic soda and EDC alter economics [11] while capacity restarts add supply. Gross margin is 7% [18]; Chlor Alkali margin fell $344.1M on caustic pricing [19], Epoxy fell $13.8M [17], Winchester fell $173.5M [15]. Tariff-driven metal inflation [13][14] can push Winchester costs higher. Caustic and ammunition margins falling together would re-rate earnings down from a low base.
