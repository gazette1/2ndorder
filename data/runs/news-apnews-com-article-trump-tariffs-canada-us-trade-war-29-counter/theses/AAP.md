# AAP: Tariff refunds are a one-time cost benefit, not a volume recovery

## Bear case first
The strongest bear reading: the $26M refund [13] is a one-time recovery, 0.3% of $8.601B revenue (SEC XBRL), and management says future recoveries are not material [14]. Insider activity is net negative, with $390.8K in selling and 0 buys (Form 4), and the customer graph shows $0 in federal awards (USASpending) and no named enterprise customers (Customer graph). The filing describes refunds of tariffs on existing products [13] but no new segment, no investment program, and no volume mechanism, consistent with the 17/100 composite score (Composite score).

## Business today
AAP is a North American automotive aftermarket parts provider serving professional installers and DIY customers [3], operating under the Advance Auto Parts and Carquest brands [6], with 4,311 stores primarily in the US and additional locations in Canada, Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands [5]. Revenue was $8.601B, cash $3.123B (SEC XBRL), net income $55M for the twelve weeks ended July 18, 2026 [1].

## Chain position
AAP occupies the retail and distribution node, with operations principally in the US and Canada [18]. After the Supreme Court overturned certain IEEPA tariffs [9], the filing shows AAP incurred tariff-related product costs [10], is eligible for direct refunds [11], and recognized refunds as a benefit to cost of sales [13]. That places AAP as a tariff payer and refund recipient, not the Canadian exporter in the seed thesis; the filing gives no volume or share-gain mechanism tied to tariff resolution.
