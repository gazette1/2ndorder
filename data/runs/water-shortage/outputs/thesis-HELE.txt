# HELE: A false positive for the water shortage thesis

## Bear case first
The filing evidence ties HELE to water only through litigation. The company is named in patent infringement litigation and an ITC action over its PUR gravity-fed water filtration systems [0][1][2][3]. The filings do not show any desalination plant, advanced treatment project, or membrane component contract. The dossier shows zero open-market insider activity (Form 4), no named customers (SEC XBRL), and no federal awards (USASpending). The disconfirming read is that HELE is a consumer filtration products company with a legal overhang, not a supplier to water infrastructure.

## Business today
Helen of Troy's PUR gravity-fed water filtration systems are the subject of the patent litigation [0]. The company reported revenue of $1.8B and cash of $18.9M (SEC XBRL). The filings do not identify the end buyer for the PUR systems beyond the litigation descriptions.

## Chain position
The map position for this thesis was order 3, the component supply node for desalination and advanced treatment plants. The filing evidence describes only PUR gravity-fed water filtration systems, which are consumer products, not membrane modules or energy-recovery devices [0]. The front-loaded component demand logic does not follow from the evidence.

## Customer graph
No named customers are present (SEC XBRL). Federal awards are $0 (USASpending). Fourteen other filers name the company (SEC XBRL), but none are identified as customers in the excerpts. The buyer base is unproven for this theme.

## Insider and management signal
Form 4 shows no open-market insider buys or sells over the trailing 12 months, which is a zero signal (Form 4). Management language in the filing is limited to litigation updates and contains no forward-looking statements about water shortage opportunities [0].

## Street view
Sell-side data is absent: analyst count unknown, consensus n/a, mean target n/a (street view). No firm ratings are available. Thin coverage is a neutral setup, not evidence of undervaluation.

## Non-linear case
A non-linear long case would need water scarcity to drive consumer demand for PUR gravity-fed filters and for the pending litigation to resolve without a costly import ban [0][1][2][3]. It would also require management to convert that consumer position into a role in desalination or advanced treatment, which the filings do not mention. The path to a multiple of today's business is not visible in the evidence; it is an open question.

## Evidence
- The company faces District Court patent litigation in the Western District of Texas over its PUR gravity-fed water filtration systems [0].
- The ITC action was brought against Kaz USA, Helen of Troy, and five other unrelated companies selling water filtration systems [1].
- The ITC complaint alleged patent infringement by the company over a limited set of PUR gravity-fed water filtration systems [2].
- Brita LP requested the ITC to initiate an unfair import investigation relating to such filtration systems [3].

## Sizing and survivability
Average daily dollar volume is $13.7M, so a small fund could build a position in about 18 trading days (reality check). Net cash is negative at -$766.7M, and share count is up 1.5% in 12 months (reality check). These balance sheet and dilution facts threaten a long thesis.

## What would change our mind
A 10-K or 10-Q that discloses a desalination or advanced treatment component segment with revenue or a named customer would be needed. Form 4 open-market insider buys would also change the signal. A USASpending record showing federal water infrastructure awards to the company would be direct evidence.
