# LNN: Water-shortage software is the hook, but the filing is an equipment story

## Bear case first
The filing shows an equipment business in cyclical decline: demand is tempered by input costs [1] and commodity weakness [6]. Irrigation revenue fell 7%, North America fell 11%, and operating income fell 25% [4][5][8]. The only disclosed backlog increase is tied to a MENA project that is equipment, not software [9][12]. The software brands are listed in a company description [14] but receive no investment, revenue, or customer discussion. With no named enterprise customer (Customer graph) and no open-market insider buying (Form 4), the theme is not validated.

## Business today
Lindsay sells irrigation and infrastructure equipment and technology to farmers and project customers [6][9][13]. Irrigation segment revenue was $133.0M in fiscal Q3 2026, down 7% [4]. Annual revenue was $659.1M and cash was $250.6M at 2025-08-31 (SEC XBRL). The irrigation brands include FieldNET, FieldWise, and FieldNET Advisor [14].

## Chain position
Lindsay occupies order 2 on the water-shortage adoption chain: irrigation software and analytics. Hardware adoption pulls demand for decision support that converts sensor readings into irrigation prescriptions. The economic logic is recurring subscription fees and sticky renewal from compliance and yield-loss avoidance. The filing does not quantify this recurring base.

## Customer graph
Government: federal awards total $100.7M (USASpending), with $43.2M and $10.0M from the Department of Education and $8.2M from the Department of Defense. Enterprise: no named customer appears in the filing excerpts (Customer graph). 17 other filers name Lindsay (Customer graph). Customer evidence is thin.

## Insider and management signal
There was no open-market insider buying or selling in the trailing 12 months (Form 4). Management language centers on softness and delivery: U.S. conditions remain soft [10], Brazil is expected to return to growth [11], and the MENA project should contribute about $70M of revenue [12]. No software spending, targets, or subscription metrics are disclosed.

## Street view
Sell-side aggregators show no visible analyst count, no consensus, and no mean target (Street view). No recent firm rating actions are identified. Thin coverage is the setup.

## Non-linear case
A FieldNET Advisor software annuity could be worth a multiple of today's equipment revenue if growers renew subscriptions under water allocation rules. The filing does not disclose software revenue, so this path is hypothetical. What has to be true is that sensor analytics measurably reduce yield loss or prove compliance, and that renewals are sticky. A separate software reporting line or a disclosed subscription metric would be the first evidence. The MENA project does not test this case [12].

## Sizing and survivability
At $18.5M average daily dollar volume, a small fund needs about 14 trading days to build a position (Reality check). Net cash is $135.3M (Reality check), so no near-term balance sheet threat is visible. Share count fell 6.4% in 12 months (Reality check), so dilution is not the risk; the thesis risk is unproven software revenue.

## What would change our mind
A Form 4 showing an open-market purchase by an executive or board member. A 10-K or 10-Q that discloses software or subscription revenue or active grower users. A USASpending award that names Lindsay for a water or irrigation technology project.
