# ARTNA: Water scarcity turns wastewater operations into contracted growth

## Bear case first
The disconfirming reading: Artesian is a regulated utility with one named municipal wastewater customer [0], no federal awards (USASpending), and no named industrial buyer. Insiders sold a net $396.697K open-market over 12 months (Form 4). Permits and construction [7][8][12] prove capital spending, not contracted revenue. Filings do not quantify the industrial water treatment market or show chemical product sales. Cash flow depends on rate increases and weather [11]; net cash is negative (Reality check).

## Business today
Artesian sells regulated tariff service: water consumption, industrial wastewater services, fixed fees, and Distribution System Improvement Charges [3]. It operates three wastewater systems with 3.8 mgd capacity [1] and operates Middletown under a 20-year contract to July 2039 [0], with reclaimed water for spray irrigation [2]. Revenue was $112.941M and cash was $52K for fiscal 2025 (Fundamentals).

## Chain position
The company is the order-2 outsourced-treatment services node, not the chemical supply node. The expected logic is that plants facing water intake or discharge limits cede capital ownership and compliance risk to operators. Existing municipal contract operation [0], tariffed industrial wastewater services [3], and planned capacity additions [7][8] support that position.

## Customer graph
The only named payer is the Town of Middletown, a government buyer [0]. Federal awards are $0 (USASpending). Four other filers name Artesian (Customer graph), but no identities are disclosed. Industrial wastewater buyers are unproven; they sit inside regulated tariffs [3].

## Insider and management signal
Form 4 shows net open-market sales of $396.697K, 0 buys, 8 sales (Form 4). Management cites a $10.0M trust reimbursement [5], two construction permits [7][8], and PFAS and new wastewater plant investments [12]. Insider selling offsets the detailed management language.

## Street view
Sell-side aggregators list no analyst coverage, consensus, or mean target; reports are paywalled (Street view). The coverage vacuum means the stock likely trades without a sell-side driven catalyst base.

## Non-linear case
If the 625,000 GPD and 1,250,000 GPD facilities [7][8] are built and signed to long-term operating contracts like the Middletown contract [0], treatment services can scale into a regional compliance platform. The trust reimbursement model [5] and owned land [9] add optionality. To produce a multiple of today's utility business, Artesian must convert permits into named customers and prove contract economics in filings; that is not in evidence yet.

## Evidence
- The Middletown wastewater contract expires July 2039 [0].
- Artesian operates three wastewater systems with combined capacity of about 3.8 mgd [1].
- Middletown facilities provide reclaimed wastewater for spray irrigation [2].
- Tariff revenues include water consumption, industrial wastewater services, fixed fees, and DSIC [3].
- Artesian received a $10.0M reimbursement from the Delaware Sand and Gravel Remedial Trust [5].
- April 2024 DNREC permit allowed construction of a 625,000 GPD regional wastewater facility at SRRF [7].
- February 2026 DNREC permit allowed construction of an additional 1,250,000 GPD regional wastewater facility [8].
- Artesian Development owns two nine-acre Sussex County parcels for a water treatment and wastewater treatment facility [9].
- Operating cash flow is affected by rate increases and weather [11].
- 2026 investments include PFAS treatment upgrades and construction of new wastewater treatment plants [12].

## Sizing and survivability
Average daily dollar volume is $1.11M, so building a position takes about 221 trading days (Reality check). Net cash is negative $176.356M and reported cash is $52K (Reality check). Share count rose 1.2% in 12 months (Reality check), so dilution is modest but present; fast accumulation is not feasible.

## What would change our mind
A 10-Q or 10-K naming a new industrial or municipal customer for the 625,000 GPD or 1,250,000 GPD facilities would ease the concentration concern. Open-market insider purchases on a future Form 4 would reverse the selling signal. A USASpending federal award for Artesian's water or wastewater services would add a second government buyer.
