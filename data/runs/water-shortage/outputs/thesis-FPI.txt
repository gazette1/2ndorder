# FPI: Avoid; water shortage is a recognized risk, not a revenue driver

## Bear case first
Short case: water shortages reduce yields, tenant credit, and collateral values in FPI’s core regions [14][9]. The short case fails near-term: loans are collateralized by real estate, crops, and equipment at fixed rates with maturities up to six years [0], acquisition screens consider water availability and rights [5][6], and Form 4 shows net insider buying, not selling [Form 4]. Filings show no dated catalyst, and management has not quantified a water-related revenue line. The strongest disconfirming read is that FPI already absorbed West Coast impairments [16], yet the allowance for credit losses remains only $0.2M [1], suggesting current stress is not acute.

## Business today
FPI owns farmland, leases it to tenant farmers, and originates collateralized loans under the FPI Loan Program [0][9]. It bears costs for major capital improvements such as irrigation systems, drainage tile, and grain storage on owned properties [3]. Revenue was $52.178M and cash was $7.654M as of 2025-12-31 [XBRL].

## Chain position
FPI sits upstream of regional banks in the water-shortage adoption chain: its land values and loan losses are an early read on collateral values that later drive bank provisions. The map position is order 3, at-risk, with a long horizon. Its credit-loss allowance and impairments are the observable transmission mechanism [1][16].

## Customer graph
Payments come from tenant farmers and loan borrowers, not from named enterprise contracts [9][0]. Federal awards to FPI are $0, no named customer appears in the excerpts, and six other filers name FPI [USASpending]. The customer base is unproven as an enterprise or government franchise; the visible buyers are dispersed agricultural tenants and borrowers.

## Insider and management signal
Trailing 12-month Form 4 filings show $195,478 of net open-market buying across 5 buys and 0 sales by 3 insiders [Form 4]. Management language is more cautious: water availability is part of due diligence, and irrigation investment is possible, but no committed capital or dedicated water program is disclosed [5][6][7]. This reads as modest insider conviction and low management conviction on water as a business line.

## Street view
Sell-side coverage is not visible: aggregators show no analyst count, consensus, or mean target for FPI [Street view]. No firm rating actions are reported. Thin coverage means the market is not pricing a near-term water catalyst.

## Non-linear case
If a persistent shortage hits California, Illinois, Colorado, and Arkansas, appraisals reset lower and tenant equity disappears [14]. Variable-rent leases and leases not requiring full advance payment pass stress directly to FPI’s income statement [9]. The allowance for credit losses would rise from $0.2M [1], and additional West Coast impairments would follow the 2025 pattern [16]. Each loss reduces book value and borrowing capacity, raising the chance that FPI must sell farmland into a falling market, which compounds the decline.

## Evidence
- [0] FPI seeks to make loans collateralized by farm real estate, crops, equipment, and other collateral, with fixed rates and maturities up to six years.
- [1] Allowance for credit losses on financing receivables was $0.2M at June 30, 2026 and December 31, 2025.
- [3] FPI bears costs for major capital improvements such as irrigation systems, drainage tile, grain storage, permanent plantings, or other structures.
- [4] The increase in allowance for credit losses was due to updated assumptions and increases in principal balances under the FPI Loan Program.
- [5] FPI seeks farmland where water availability through precipitation and irrigation meets agronomic needs.
- [6] Acquisition due diligence evaluates water availability and associated ground or surface water rights.
- [7] FPI may invest in irrigation infrastructure to improve the productivity of properties it owns.
- [9] FPI is exposed to tenant credit risk and farming operation risks, including water shortages, on leases that do not require 100% advance payment, variable-rent leases, and leases longer than one year.
- [14] FPI is particularly susceptible to adverse weather, water availability, and growing conditions in California, Illinois, Colorado, and Arkansas, which generate a significant portion of revenues.
- [16] Impairment in 2025 related to West Coast properties; 2024 impairment related to trade names of Murray Wise Associates and irrigation assets held for sale.

## Sizing and survivability
Average daily dollar volume is $4.28M, so building a full position takes about 58 trading days [reality check]. Net cash is -$153.937M, and cash is $7.654M, so the balance sheet relies on debt and operations, not a cash cushion [XBRL; reality check]. Share count is down 1.2% over 12 months, which lowers dilution risk; no shelf is cited.

## What would change our mind
A 10-Q or 10-K showing the allowance for credit losses stable or falling despite continuing drought would weaken the collateral-loss thesis. A Form 4 cluster of open-market purchases by insiders after a share-price decline would also count against a short. A federal award to FPI for water infrastructure visible in USASpending would create the customer validation that is currently absent.
