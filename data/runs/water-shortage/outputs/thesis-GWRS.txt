# GWRS: Source cost inflation outruns ACC rate approvals, compressing coverage until delayed rate cases close

## Bear case first
The short case is that source costs rise faster than allowed rates and coverage ratios compress [19]. The short fails because the ACC has already approved or settled the material cases: GW-Farmers was approved [7], GW-Santa Cruz settled [4], and the Farmers acquisition premium stays eligible for future rate base [8]. The filings do not show a cash shortfall; cash was $4.08M and insiders bought $221.8K with no sales (SEC XBRL, Form 4). The filings do not show the actual coverage ratio, so margin compression cannot be quantified from this evidence.

## Business today
Regulated water and wastewater utility in Arizona. Revenue was $55.8M and cash was $4.08M (SEC XBRL). Rates are a fixed fee plus a volumetric fee [17], and rate changes require ACC approval [18]. Acquired seven Tucson Water systems for about $8.1M [1].

## Chain position
Rate-regulated water utility credit, order 2, at-risk. Source costs rise faster than allowed rates, regulators approve with lag, and capital programs are funded while returns are delayed [19] [15].

## Customer graph
No named enterprise customers. Federal awards total $125K from the Department of the Interior (USASpending). Five other filers name the company. The only named transaction counterparty is the City of Tucson [1]. Customer evidence is thin.

## Insider and management signal
Four insiders bought $221.8K net, zero sales (Form 4). Management cites specific rate cases, a settlement, an $8.1M acquisition, and Total Water Management [1] [14] [4]. The buying conflicts with the negative net cash position, which the analyst should weigh.

## Street view
No sell-side coverage: no analysts, no consensus, no target (sell-side aggregators). Thin coverage is part of the setup for a $206M cap name.

## Non-linear case
If Arizona water scarcity drives source costs up faster than the ACC allows, and the GW-Palo Verde refile in 2027 [4] delays recovery further, the settlement's preserved rate base inclusion [5] becomes a future option, not current cash flow. With net cash at negative $129.6M and a shelf on file (reality check), debt-funded acquisitions or an equity draw would compound losses. The non-linear outcome is a credit event that widens spreads and cuts equity returns.
