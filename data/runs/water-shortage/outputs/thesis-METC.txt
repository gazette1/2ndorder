# METC: Avoid. Water scarcity is an excluded input cost at the Brook Mine critical minerals project.

## Bear case first
The short thesis is that water scarcity breaks project economics, but the disclosure weakens that case. The process design recycles water: thickener overflow and centrate are recycled [18], MVR-recovered process water is redistributed [9], and Thickening and Dewatering recovers as much mineral process water as possible [16]. The report assumes treated raw water is sufficient for potable water [11], and raw water supply is excluded from the Hatch scope [1]. No insider selling appears in Form 4, and no customers are named. The filings do not quantify water tariffs, basin stress, or curtailment probability, so the short relies on inference.

## Business today
The 8-K describes the Brook Mine Critical Minerals Project process definition, including leach tanks, filters, centrifuges, and gas scrubbers [8], and it includes an overview of process water usage and recovery [2]. It does not state what the company sells or to whom. SEC XBRL reports revenue of $24.0M and cash of $440.3M as of 2017-12-31, while the current reality check shows net cash of -$27.2M; the evidence does not reconcile the dates.

## Chain position
METC occupies the water-intensive manufacturing node of the water-shortage adoption chain, order 1, at risk, near horizon. Process water and cooling water are embedded inputs [4][5], and water from Thickening and Dewatering is recovered in the Mineral Process Water System [19]. Scarcity raises operating costs and can cap output where permits restrict withdrawals.

## Non-linear case
The downside path begins with observed high water and reagent consumption [0] meeting a stressed basin. Raw water supply is outside project scope [1], so the company must procure it at an unstated cost. If permits are curtailed or tariffs rise, the project must recycle more, with the energy cost that recovery implies [17], or cap throughput. The shelf registration lets the company fund overruns with equity, compounding dilution. What has to be true is that the basin is physically stressed and the project reaches water delivery, which the evidence does not confirm.

## Evidence
- High reagent and water consumption were observed in subsequent testing [0].
- Raw water supply is excluded from the Hatch project scope [1].
- The report includes an overview of process water usage and recovery [2].
- Cooling water systems remove excess heat from heat exchangers, de-sublimators, and distillation columns [4].
- Process water is used as make-up source to the cooling tower [5].
- Process water is distributed at 50 C to de-sublimators, filters, centrifuges, leach tanks, gas scrubbers, and the mineral process water system [8].
- Process water from de-sublimators and recovered from MVR systems is cooled and redistributed [9].
- Treated raw water is assumed sufficient for potable water [11].
- Thickening and Dewatering recovers as much mineral process water as possible [16].
- This step is essential to minimizing fresh process water demand and decreasing energy consumption [17].
- Thickener overflow and centrifuge centrate are sent to the Mineral Process Water System to recycle the water [18].
- Water from Thickening and Dewatering is recovered in the Mineral Process Water System [19].
