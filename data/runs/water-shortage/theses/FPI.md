# FPI: Water shortage is a collateral risk without a monetized water business

## Bear case first
Water shortage is disclosed as a risk and due diligence factor, not a revenue line [5][6][9]. The filings do not show a quantified water strategy, named customer, or dated catalyst. That absence protects a short from being early, because impairments and allowance builds can be spread over several periods; the allowance is only $0.2MM [1]. Insider buying is modest, so the bear case rests on slow collateral erosion.

## Business today
FPI owns farmland used for crops [10] and originates loans collateralized by farm and non-farm real estate, crops, and equipment, with $1.0M or more principal and maturities up to six years [0]. It bears costs for major improvements such as irrigation systems [3]. Revenue $52,178M, cash $7,654M (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
FPI is the collateral and credit node in the farm real estate chain, ahead of regional banks with farm exposure. Its loans are backed by farmland [0], and its tenants carry credit risk tied to water shortages [9]. Persistent shortage reduces collateral values and tenant cash flows, pressuring FPI and bank lenders with farm exposure together.

## Customer graph
No named enterprise customer and no federal awards appear (USASpending). Payers are tenants and borrowers, but the excerpts name none; tenant credit risk is disclosed [9] and loans are described generally [0]. Buyer type is unproven. Six other filers name FPI, without contract dollars.

## Insider and management signal
Trailing 12-month open-market insider activity was net $195M across 5 buys and 0 sales by 3 insiders (Form 4). Management treats water as underwriting: due diligence includes water availability [6], and irrigation investment is only a possible productivity improvement [7]. No committed water program is disclosed.

## Street view
No analyst count, consensus, or mean target is available (street view). Reports are paywalled. Thin coverage leaves the credit story under-analyzed until filings force a revision.

## Non-linear case
If water shortage persists in California, Illinois, Colorado, and Arkansas, the regions FPI says generate significant revenue [14], appraisals reset lower and the $0.2MM allowance [1] proves insufficient. The 10-K already shows a West Coast impairment and irrigation asset impairment [16]. With net cash at -$153,937M (reality check), FPI could cut the dividend or issue equity. A bank-led repricing of farmland collateral would turn slow erosion into a downward step.

## Evidence
- [0] Loans: collateralized by farm/non-farm real estate, crops, equipment; $1.0M+; fixed rates; up to six years.
- [1] Allowance for credit losses on financing receivables was $0.2MM at June 30, 2026 and December 31, 2025.
- [3] Company bears costs for irrigation systems and other major capital improvements.
- [5] Seeks farmland where water availability through precipitation and irrigation meets crop needs.
- [6] Due diligence evaluates water availability and water rights.
- [7] May invest in irrigation infrastructure to improve productivity.
- [9] Tenant credit risk includes water shortages and adverse weather.
- [10] Farmland is typically used for growing crops.
- [14] Significant revenue from California, Illinois, Colorado, and Arkansas; exposed to water availability.
- [16] 2025 impairment on West Coast properties; 2024 impairment of trade names and irrigation assets held for sale.

## Sizing and survivability
Average daily dollar volume is $4,058M, about 9 trading days to build a position (reality check). Cash is $7,654M against net cash -$153,937M, leaving no cushion. Share count is down 1.2% in 12 months, but a downside scenario makes future equity issuance likely.

## What would change our mind
A 10-Q or 10-K that shows a water-related revenue line or named customer would close the revenue gap. A Form 4 with purchases materially above the trailing $195M net would signal asset-value conviction. A federal award or named government contract would add customer validation.
