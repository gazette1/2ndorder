# ARTNA: Water scarcity converts wastewater treatment contracts into recurring, compliance-driven revenue

## Bear case first
Insiders sold $0.4M on the open market over 12 months with zero buys (Form 4). The revenue base is still a regulated utility, and the industrial treatment theme is an extension, not a new segment. Middletown is the only named customer, and federal awards are $0 (USASpending). Cash of $52M against net debt of $176.4MM leaves little headroom, and the filings name no industrial payer.

## Business today
Revenue is $112.9MM and cash is $52M as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL). Artesian sells regulated water and wastewater services: consumption tariffs, industrial wastewater services, fixed fees, service charges, and Distribution System Improvement Charges [3][4]. It operates three wastewater systems totaling about 3.8 mgd, including the 20-year Middletown contract, and supplies reclaimed water for spray irrigation [1][0][2].

## Chain position
Artesian is a second-order beneficiary in industrial water treatment services. Water shortage forces facilities into higher-cycle cooling, recycling, and discharge compliance, and operators outsource chemistry and operations to meet permits. Contracts extend because operators prefer guaranteed compliance to owning treatment assets.

## Customer graph
Town of Middletown (government) is the only named customer [0]. Federal awards are $0 (USASpending). Four other filers name the company, but no industrial buyer is named; the tariff for industrial wastewater services [3] implies such buyers, but their revenue is unproven.

Selling insiders against investing management is the tension.

## Non-linear case
The two DNREC permits add 1.875 mgd of capacity, about a 50 percent expansion over 3.8 mgd, and the Sussex parcels allow more buildout [1][7][8][9]. If these become long-term municipal or industrial contracts on the Middletown model, Artesian shifts from utility returns to contracted service returns. The $10.0M trust reimbursement proves outside capital funds contamination treatment [5]. The permits are dated milestones; the contracts are not yet evidenced.

## Evidence
- Middletown wastewater operations run under a 20-year contract expiring July 2039 [0].
- Three systems provide combined capacity of about 3.8 mgd [1].
- Tariff revenues include industrial wastewater services [3].
- Water consumption revenue is recognized on metered volume at tariff rates [4].
- Middletown provides reclaimed wastewater for spray irrigation [2].
- About $10.0M received from the Delaware Sand and Gravel Remedial Trust for treatment costs [5].
- DNREC permitted a 625,000 GPD facility in April 2024 [7].
- DNREC permitted a 1,250,000 GPD facility in February 2026 [8].
- Two nine-acre Sussex County parcels are owned for treatment construction [9].
- 2026 capex includes PFAS treatment upgrades and new wastewater plants [12].

## Sizing and survivability
Daily dollar volume is $1.2MM, about 28 trading days to build a position. Cash is $52M against net debt of $176.4MM, and the share count is up 1.2 percent in 12 months (SEC XBRL). Dilution is modest, but the cash position makes debt or equity likely for expansion.

## What would change our mind
