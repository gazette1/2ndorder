# METC: Water scarcity is an input risk, not a revenue driver; avoid the short until the downside path has filing evidence.

## Bear case first
The short case fails because the filings do not establish water scarcity as a business threat. Raw water supply is excluded from the project scope [1], so the company is not taking ownership of that risk. High reagent and water consumption in testing [0] is a process development issue, not evidence of curtailment. Dossier data show net $0 open-market insider buying or selling (Form 4) and no federal awards or named customers (USASpending, customer graph), so the usual red flags are absent. Thin customer evidence cuts against modeling a revenue impact, which is a reason to avoid rather than short.

## Business today
The July 2026 8-K includes an Initial Assessment Report for the Brook Mine Critical Minerals Project, describing a mineral process plant with water distribution and recovery systems [7][8][16]. SEC XBRL as of 2017-12-31 reports revenue of $24.019MM and cash of $440.347MM, a dated snapshot.

## Chain position
METC occupies the at-risk node for water-intensive manufacturing: process water is used for slurrying, cooling, and separation [4][13]. The project is designed to recycle water [18][19], which lowers demand but does not create a water-scarcity product or revenue line. Raw water supply is outside project scope [1], leaving the company dependent on off-site delivery.

## Customer graph
No named enterprise customers are disclosed (unproven). Federal awards total $0 (USASpending). No other filers name the company (customer graph). Customer evidence is too thin to validate either a long or a short thesis.

## Insider and management signal
Form 4 shows no open-market insider activity in the trailing 12 months, a neutral not positive signal (Form 4). Management identifies water recovery as essential to minimizing fresh process water demand [17], but the report names no committed capital or program.

## Street view
Sell-side coverage is unknown: consensus and mean target are n/a (sell-side aggregators). No firm or rating can be identified. For a small-cap with no visible analyst challenge, the water-scarcity story is vulnerable to narrative swings.

## Non-linear case
Downside compounds if the Brook Mine region imposes water withdrawal limits. The assessment assumes treated raw water is sufficient for potable use [11] and excludes raw water supply from its scope [1], so a curtailment would hit a plan with no owned buffer. High reagent and water consumption [0] would raise operating costs per ton. Water recovery [16][18] can mitigate, but it cannot offset a full shut-in. The shelf registration on file (reality check) could convert a cost overrun into equity dilution.

## Evidence
- Raw water supply is excluded from project scope [1]; treated raw water is assumed sufficient for potable use [11].
- Subsequent testing observed high reagent and water consumption [0].
- Thickening and dewatering recovers as much mineral process water as possible [16]; this step is essential to minimizing fresh process water demand [17].
- Cooling water systems remove excess heat [4]; mineral process water is added to slurry ROM to 65 wt.% solids [13].
- Process water is distributed at 50 C to de-sublimators, filters, centrifuges, leach tanks, gas scrubbers, and the mineral process water system [8].
- Process water and condensate are distributed throughout the plant [7].
- Water from Thickening and Dewatering is recovered in the Mineral Process Water System [19]; thickener overflow and centrate are recycled [18].

## What would change our mind
A Form 4 showing open-market selling by an insider would add a negative conviction signal. An 8-K or registration statement disclosing a water withdrawal permit limit, curtailment, or water tariff increase at the Brook Mine would validate the downside mechanism. A federal award or a named customer for a water-related project would instead show a revenue link and reduce the at-risk water exposure.
