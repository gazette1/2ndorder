# SBFG: Short or avoid as water shortage pressures farm credit, while the balance sheet protects near term

## Bear case first
The short thesis fails near term because agricultural exposure is only 16.1 percent of the HFI portfolio [4], with nine consecutive quarters of loan portfolio expansion [0] and credit results showing "excellent stability" [1]. Non-mortgage loan sales of small business and farm credits were just $1.0MM in 2025 [9], so farm credit is retained. Insider net selling is small at $35.6M (Form 4), with one buy against two sales, and the customer graph shows no named customers or federal awards to attack (customer graph, USASpending). The filings do not mention water shortage.

## Business today
SBFG operates State Bank, providing wealth management, private client services, mortgage banking, and commercial and agricultural lending through 27 offices in Ohio and Indiana [2]. Revenue is not reported in XBRL; cash was $149.5MM as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL). The loan portfolio is 50.6 percent commercial real estate, 25.8 percent residential first mortgages, 16.1 percent commercial business and agricultural, and 7.5 percent consumer [4][5].

## Chain position
SBFG sits on the farm credit and agricultural lending node, order 2, at risk, horizon mid. Water shortage lowers crop revenue and forces herd liquidation, reducing debt service, while water rights curtailment lowers collateral values of irrigated land. Loss severity can exceed the income shortfall because farmland appraisal ties value to sustainable water access.

## Street view
Sell-side coverage is zero, with no consensus and no mean target (street view). The coverage gap is part of the setup: no analyst is modeling water risk into the agricultural book, so the stock can reprice when the theme hits.

## Non-linear case
The downside compounds if water shortage becomes a credit event. Crop revenue declines and herd liquidation raise delinquencies on the 16.1 percent agricultural book [4], while the 50.6 percent commercial real estate book [4], secured by real property [3], weakens. Farmland collateral falls when appraisal discounts land without sustainable water access, and the allowance [6] proves inadequate as nonperforming assets stop resolving [1]. If losses exceed earnings, the 4 percent share count decline (reality check) reverses into a dilutive capital raise. The equity trades to a fraction of today's market cap.
