# GWRS: Regulatory lag strains coverage as source costs outrun ACC-approved rates

## Bear case first
The short thesis is that source costs rise faster than ACC-approved rates during the rate-case lag [18][19]. What protects GWRS: the April 2026 settlement settled GW-Santa Cruz and deferred GW-Palo Verde to a 2027 refiling [4], and the ACC approved the GW-Farmers rate case in April 2025 [6][7], showing rates eventually reset. Insiders bought $221.849M open-market with zero sales (Form 4), and the filings do not quantify the cost-revenue gap. Customer evidence is thin but positive: $125M in federal awards (USASpending) and no named enterprise customers.

## Business today
GWRS sells rate-regulated water and wastewater service in Arizona, charging a fixed fee plus a volumetric fee [17]. Revenue was $55.758MM and cash was $4.080MM at 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL). The company closed an $8.1MM acquisition of seven Tucson Water systems in July 2025 [1][16] and serves the Phoenix MSA [15].

## Chain position
GWRS occupies order 2 of the water-shortage chain: rate-regulated water utility credit. Phoenix MSA growth [15] drives connections and rate base, but ACC approval [18] and rate cases that take a year or more [19] delay rate relief. Initial testimony was filed October 2025 [3]; the April 2026 settlement deferred GW-Palo Verde to 2027 [4].

## Customer graph
Buyers are Arizona ratepayers, not named enterprise customers. Government awards total $125M from the Department of the Interior: $75M (R22AP00396), $50M (R25AP00184), and $0 (R22AP00411) (USASpending). Five other SEC filers name GWRS. Enterprise: none named. Ratepayer base: unproven.

## Insider and management signal

Management names specific rate cases [2], a settlement agreement [4], the $8.1MM acquisition [1], and Total Water Management [14]. No quantified growth targets appear.

## Street view

## Non-linear case
The downside path compounds if the GW-Palo Verde withdrawal [4] becomes a long delay or a denied refiling. Net cash is -$129.618MM and share count rose 18.7% in 12 months (Reality check), so equity funding is likely. A coverage breach would force a dilutive raise at a depressed price, and each delay widens the gap between source costs and revenue [19]. The settlement preserves rate-base recovery only in a future case [5], and bill credits [9][10] and viability conditions [8] show the ACC can delay recovery.

## What would change our mind
