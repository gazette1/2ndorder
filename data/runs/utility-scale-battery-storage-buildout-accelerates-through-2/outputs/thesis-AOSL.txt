# AOSL: Utility-scale battery storage buildout accelerates through 2028

## Bear case first
The strongest disconfirming reading: AOSL already sells IGBTs, SiC, and power modules into multiple end markets, and storage is not a disclosed segment. Filings do not quantify storage revenue or name a storage inverter customer. Insider open-market selling of $2.0M across 19 sales, compared with one buy, points to insider conviction near zero (Form 4). The theme may simply re-rate an existing product line without a visible catalyst.

## Business today
AOSL designs and sells a broad portfolio of power semiconductors [1]. The portfolio includes power discretes and power ICs [16], about 2,800 products with over 100 new products introduced in fiscal 2025 [2], including trench MOSFETs, trench-stop IGBTs, and application-specific MOSFETs [18], plus 1200V SiC products for solar inverters, UPS, and battery management systems [17]. Revenue was $696.2M and cash was $153.1M as of 2025-06-30 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
AOSL is a power semiconductor and module supplier, order 2 in the adoption chain. Inverter manufacturers buy IGBT and SiC modules for power conversion, and modules are a high-value, constrained component inside every storage inverter. As inverter production scales, module suppliers gain volume leverage and can pass through shortages more effectively than downstream assemblers.

## Customer graph
Named enterprise customers: none in the evidence. Government: $0 in federal awards (USASpending). Two other SEC filers name AOSL, but the relationship is unproven. The customer evidence is thin across all buyer types.

## Insider and management signal
One insider sold $2.0M net on the open market over 12 months, with one buy and 19 sales (Form 4). Management language is general: it describes leveraging power semiconductor expertise [15] and deploying SiC for battery management systems [17], but gives no storage-specific targets or named programs. Management unveiled the mega IPM-7 intelligent power module series in Q4 fiscal 2025 [19] without tying it to storage.

## Street view
No sell-side coverage data is available; analyst count, consensus, and mean target are n/a. For a $950M market cap name with $31.1M average daily dollar volume, thin coverage is part of the setup.

## Non-linear case
If utility-scale storage inverter production grows faster than module supply, AOSL could gain volume leverage and pricing power. The 39.2% interest in a Chongqing packaging and 12-inch wafer fab may provide capacity and cost advantage [5]. For a multiple to occur, storage must move from one of several applications to the primary revenue driver, with disclosed customer programs and qualification wins. The path requires a visible increase in named storage customers.

## Evidence

- Power discretes and power ICs as the two major product categories [16].
- Approximately 2,800 products; over 100 new products in fiscal 2025 [2].
- Power discretes include trench MOSFETs, trench-stop IGBTs, and application-specific MOSFETs [18].
- 1200V SiC on AlphaSiC platform for solar inverters, UPS, and battery management systems [17].
- Strategy to leverage power semiconductor expertise [15].
- Mega IPM-7 intelligent power modules unveiled in Q4 fiscal 2025 [19].
- 39.2% equity interest in a Chongqing packaging, testing, and 12-inch wafer fab joint venture [5].

## Sizing and survivability
A small fund can build a position in about 8 trading days at the $31.1M average daily dollar volume (reality check). Net cash is $138.2M against revenue of $696.2M, and share count rose 0.6 percent in 12 months, so dilution risk is low. A shelf filing is not stated in the evidence; that remains an open question.

## What would change our mind
- A 10-Q or 8-K disclosing a named storage inverter customer or a qualification milestone for IGBT or SiC modules.
- Form 4 filings showing sustained open-market buying instead of selling.
- A USASpending federal award to AOSL for storage-related power modules or a disclosed multi-year supply agreement.
