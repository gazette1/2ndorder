# WOLF: Utility-scale battery storage buildout through 2028 pulls SiC power module demand to Wolfspeed

## Bear case first
The filings never mention battery storage. The only triggers are a leadership appointment and vague language about "strategic priorities" and "compelling long-term opportunities" [1], with no named storage customers, design-win dates, or quantified TAM. Wolfspeed holds $1,220.3M net debt and about 10.3 quarters of cash runway (Reality check), so a capital raise likely lands before the 2028 peak. Insider activity is zero (Form 4), and the customer graph is DoD awards, not utility inverters (USASpending). The thesis fails if inverter makers do not choose Wolfspeed modules under shortage conditions.

## Business today
Wolfspeed sells silicon carbide material, power modules, discrete power devices and power die products for electrification, energy efficiency and advanced industrial applications [2][5]. Revenue was $757.6M and cash $467.2M as of 2025-06-29 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
Wolfspeed occupies the power semiconductor and module supplier node, one step above inverter assemblers. Storage inverters require high-value IGBT and silicon carbide modules; as inverter production scales to match the battery buildout, module suppliers gain volume leverage and pass through shortages more effectively than downstream assemblers. Order 2 beneficiary, mid horizon.

## Non-linear case
The multiple path requires silicon carbide to displace IGBTs in storage inverters, with Wolfspeed's vertical integration from material to module [5] capturing margin across the stack. Tight SiC supply in a 2028 buildout could price modules above cost curves and double the $757.6M revenue base (SEC XBRL). This requires survival to 2028; the $1,220.3M net debt (Reality check) makes that the crux. If survival is solved, the equity is worth multiples; if not, the thesis is moot.
