# CBNK: Avoid, the battery storage buildout bypasses a bank with no disclosed pumped-hydro exposure

## Bear case first
The short thesis rests on battery storage compressing pumped-hydro economics, but CBNK's filings show no connection to pumped storage, energy lending, or any storage program [0]. The strongest negative signals are net insider selling of -$1.4M with zero buys (Form 4) and federal awards of $3.0M that are immaterial against a $608M market cap. Those signals are too thin to support a high-conviction short. What protects the company is that the theme has no measurable attachment point in the evidence.

## Business today
The company is a bank, but the available excerpt of its investor overview contains no business description; the only text is an EIA definitional footnote [0]. Revenue is not reported in the XBRL data, and cash is $34.7M (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
The mapped node is pumped hydro storage operators, order 1, at risk. CBNK's position in that node is unproven; no filing describes lending to, financing from, or revenue shared with any pumped-storage operator. The economic logic for a bank would be project finance or deposits from storage operators, and that logic is absent from the evidence.

## Customer graph
Government: $3.0M in federal awards from the Department of the Treasury across three awards (USASpending), including $700K (161FA020805), $700K (151FA013630), and $580K (171FA021668-G). Enterprise: none named in the excerpts. Four other filers name the company (customer graph), but their identities are not shown. Customer evidence is thin and entirely government-sourced.

## Insider and management signal
Trailing 12 months show net open-market selling of -$1.4M across 2 sales and 0 buys by 0 named insiders (Form 4); the zero-insider attribution is a data discrepancy, not a confirmed insider change. Management language is absent from the provided excerpts; the only filing text is an external EIA footnote, not management commentary [0].

## Street view
Sell-side coverage is unquantified: no analysts, no consensus, and no mean target are available (street view). For a $608M market cap bank, the absence of coverage removes both a validation channel and a source of forecast revisions.

## Non-linear case
The downside outcome is not a storage-led impairment, because the bank has no demonstrable energy book. The compounding path is negative selection in the loan book: if the Treasury award relationship signals a narrow specialty, and if battery-tied power price volatility degrades a borrower base that is not yet disclosed, credit losses could accelerate. That path requires a 10-K or 10-Q to reveal an energy or infrastructure loan concentration that the current evidence does not show, and for that exposure to default.

## Evidence
- The 8-K investor overview excerpt contains only an EIA definitional footnote listing pumped storage and batteries as electricity generation categories; no CBNK energy program is described [0].

## Sizing and survivability
A fund needs about 108 trading days to build a position at the $2.29M average daily dollar volume, so a sizable short is impractical to construct quickly (reality check). Net cash is $34.7M (SEC XBRL), and share count fell 1.7% in 12 months, so dilution is not the risk; the liquidity trap is. The short works only for investors who can accept a multi-quarter position build.

## What would change our mind
- A Form 4 showing open-market insider buys within two quarters would contradict the seller-only signal.
- A 10-K or 10-Q that discloses energy storage, pumped hydro, or infrastructure lending exposures would quantify the real at-risk asset base.
- A new federal award or a named enterprise customer in a filing would strengthen the customer graph beyond the current Treasury awards.
