Supply chain risks, including single-source reliance and quality or yield issues, could break the pass-through logic [0].

## Business today
Navitas designs, develops, and markets next-generation power semiconductors: GaN power ICs, high-voltage SiC devices, silicon system controllers, and digital isolators [2]. The company is fabless, partnering with third-party foundries and test facilities [10]. Revenue was $45.9M and cash was $236.9M as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
Navitas occupies the power semiconductor and module supplier node, an order-2 beneficiary of the storage buildout. Inverter manufacturers buy SiC and GaN modules to meet rising power-conversion demand. Module suppliers gain volume leverage and pass through shortages more effectively than downstream assemblers, if the supply chain holds [0].

## Non-linear case
The non-linear path depends on the revenue-to-opportunity mismatch. GaN and SiC were a small fraction of total power semiconductors shipped in 2025 [6], while Navitas claims a pure-play position at the intersection of AI data center growth and grid modernization [7]. If storage inverters scale and SiC modules become constrained, high-value components, Navitas gains volume leverage and shortage pass-through. Revenue is small relative to the opportunity, so BESS wins could move the whole company. That requires design wins, qualifications, and foundry capacity, none of which appear in the filings. The full-stack GaN plus SiC portfolio [8] and patent base [9][14] support the position.
