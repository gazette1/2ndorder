# ASYS: SiC wafer equipment demand follows utility-scale storage buildout through 2028

## Bear case first
The primary disconfirming read is that Amtech sells capital equipment used in the earliest stages of wafer manufacturing, not silicon carbide wafers or power modules, so an inverter-led SiC demand cycle only matters if wafer makers add capacity. The 10-K identifies SiC as an application [1] but does not quantify revenue, market size, backlog, design wins, qualification milestones, capacity additions, or named customer programs. Form 4 shows no insider buying to support conviction (Form 4). Federal customer evidence is $892.3K (USASpending), leaving the commercial customer base unproven. A 22.3% share count increase and an active shelf raise the risk that any eventual success is diluted (reality check).

## Business today
Amtech sells products used in the earliest stages of silicon or silicon carbide wafer manufacturing, then in semiconductor device manufacturing, advanced packaging, and electronics assembly [0]. Common applications include silicon wafers for digital and analog devices, SiC wafers for power devices, optics, and ceramics [1]. Revenue was $79.4M and cash was $17.9M as of 2025-09-30 (SEC XBRL); specific customers are not named in the excerpts.

## Chain position
Amtech sits upstream of silicon carbide wafer suppliers as a capital equipment provider. In the adoption thesis, power module makers increase silicon carbide wafer purchases for higher-efficiency inverters, and inverter product-line upgrades push wafer demand ahead of module shipments. Amtech is an order-3 beneficiary: it would see demand only when wafer suppliers decide to expand capacity.

## Street view
Sell-side analyst coverage is unknown, with no consensus and no mean target. No firm, grade, or recent rating action appears in the evidence. For an under-covered small-cap, thin coverage is itself part of the setup.

## Non-linear case
Utility-scale battery storage buildout through 2028 could force inverter makers to qualify and buy SiC power modules in volume. If that happens, wafer suppliers must add capacity, and Amtech's equipment is key to the earliest stages of wafer manufacturing [0]. The non-linear outcome is an equipment order cycle that turns a $79.4M revenue base into a multiple of itself (SEC XBRL). This requires SiC wafer demand to tighten enough that wafer makers commit to new factories, and it requires Amtech to win a share of those orders.

## What would change our mind
A 10-K with a segment or product-line disclosure that quantifies SiC-related revenue would strengthen the revenue-to-opportunity case. A Form 4 open-market insider purchase would add conviction. A new federal award naming Amtech, or an 8-K disclosure of a named commercial customer, would broaden the customer graph.
