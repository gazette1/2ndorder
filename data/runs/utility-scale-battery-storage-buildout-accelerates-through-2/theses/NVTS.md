# NVTS: Utility-scale storage buildout lifts a high-power SiC module supplier, but filing evidence does not yet show orders or insider support

## Bear case first
The thesis fails if storage inverter demand grows but Navitas does not convert it into revenue. Filings describe BESS as an R&D application [13], but show no named storage customers, no design-win disclosures, and no quantified management targets. Form 4 insiders sold heavily over the trailing 12 months (source), and the customer graph shows zero federal awards and no named customers (source). Competition from Infineon, STMicroelectronics, ON Semiconductor, and Power Integrations [12], plus reliance on single-source supply with quality and capacity risks [0], makes share gains uncertain.

## Business today
Navitas designs, develops, and markets next-generation power semiconductors, including GaN power ICs, high-voltage SiC devices, associated silicon controllers, and digital isolators for power conversion and charging [2][16]. Revenue was $45.916MM and cash was $236.857MM as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL). The company positions itself at the intersection of AI data center growth and grid modernization [7].

## Chain position
Navitas occupies the supplier node of the power semiconductor adoption chain, order 2, as a beneficiary of inverter module demand. Inverter makers procure IGBT and SiC power modules, and Navitas sells GaN and high-voltage SiC products targeting energy and grid infrastructure [1][13]. Power modules are a constrained, high-value component, so suppliers can gain volume leverage as inverter production scales.

## Customer graph
Customer evidence is thin. No named enterprise customer appears in the dossier, no federal award dollars are present, and zero other filers name the company (source). The filing describes target applications but does not name a buyer of storage-related products [13][18]. The customer base is unproven from the available evidence.

## Insider and management signal
Form 4 data show open-market sales of -$156.991MM across 29 sales and zero buys, with the number of insider identities listed as zero, so the data as provided are internally inconsistent (source). Management language is directional: the Navitas 2.0 restructuring repositions the company toward high-power markets [17], but no quantified targets or committed program spending appear in the excerpts.

## Street view
Sell-side coverage is not observable in the dossier: unknown analyst count, no consensus, and no mean target (source). The absence of coverage is part of the setup for an under-covered small-cap, but it also means no external validation is visible.

## Non-linear case
The non-linear path is a storage buildout that raises SiC content per inverter because wide-bandgap devices improve cost of ownership, efficiency, size, and weight [5]. Navitas has one of the broadest IP portfolios [9][14] and is a full-stack provider of both GaN and high-voltage SiC [8]. Since GaN and SiC were only a small fraction of power semiconductors shipped in 2025 [6], even one named storage inverter design win could move the whole company. Feasibility requires that high-power storage revenue actually appears in future filings, not just in R&D descriptions.

## Sizing and survivability
A small fund can build a position quickly: average daily dollar volume is $583.908MM, roughly one trading day to complete a position (source). Net cash is $236.857MM and cash runway is about 22.1 quarters (source). A shelf registration is on file, so dilution risk exists, but the runway reduces near-term urgency.

## What would change our mind
A Form 4 showing open-market insider purchases would signal conviction. A 10-K or 8-K naming a storage inverter customer and a SiC design win would validate the mechanism. A USASpending federal award to Navitas for storage or grid applications would add government-backed demand.
