# AOSL: Power module demand from utility storage inverters rises through 2028

## Bear case first
The disconfirming reading is that AOSL has no disclosed storage-specific revenue, no named storage customers, and no quantified storage target. The 10-K describes 1200V SiC products for solar inverters and battery management and over 100 new products, but it does not tie these to battery storage or disclose design-win milestones [2][17][19]. Form 4 shows net open-market selling of $2.039184MM over trailing 12 months, with 1 buy and 19 sales by 1 insider (Form 4). The customer graph shows no named enterprise customer and zero federal awards (customer graph, USASpending). The storage thesis is inferred, not visible in filings.

## Business today
AOSL designs and supplies a broad portfolio of power semiconductors for consumer, communications, and industrial markets [1][4]. It reports extensive IP, approximately 2,800 products, over 100 new products in fiscal 2025, and power discrete and IC families including IGBTs, 1200V SiC AlphaSiC, and intelligent power modules [0][2][16][17][19]. Revenue was $696.162MM and cash was $153.079MM as of 2025-06-30 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
AOSL occupies the order-2 beneficiary node, upstream of storage inverter manufacturers. Inverters use IGBT and SiC modules for DC-to-AC conversion and grid control, and AOSL supplies those families [18][17]. The thesis is that module supply is constrained, so module suppliers gain volume leverage and pass through shortages.

## Non-linear case
If utility storage buildout accelerates through 2028, inverter module demand can grow faster than module capacity. AOSL already sells 1200V SiC modules aimed at solar inverters and battery management [17]. Its 39.2% stake in the Chongqing JV provides wafer and assembly access [5]. The non-linear outcome requires module pricing to outperform input costs and AOSL to win storage inverter module sockets.

## Evidence
- AOSL reports extensive IP and is a broad power semiconductor supplier [0][1].
- The portfolio includes approximately 2,800 products, over 100 new products in fiscal 2025, power discretes and power ICs, IGBTs, 1200V SiC AlphaSiC, trench MOSFETs, and intelligent power modules [2][16][4][17][18][19].
- AOSL owns 39.2% of a JV operating a packaging, testing, and 12-inch wafer fabrication facility in Chongqing [5] and states that a sale will provide capital for technology, R&D, and acquisitions [6].
- The strategy is to leverage power semiconductor expertise to drive new technology platforms [15].

## What would change our mind
A Form 4 with open-market buys by an insider would reverse the negative ownership signal. A 10-Q or 10-K that quantifies storage revenue or discloses named inverter customers would confirm the chain position. An 8-K or a USASpending record showing a storage module supply agreement, a qualification milestone, or a federal award would show design traction or a government buyer.
