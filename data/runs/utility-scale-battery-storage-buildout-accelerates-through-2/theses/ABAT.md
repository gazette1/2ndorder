# ABAT: Faster utility battery storage buildout feeds lithium-ion recycling feedstock through 2028

## Bear case first
The bull case depends on feedstock volume, but the company says success is fundamentally dependent on securing adequate spent batteries at economic prices [9]. The only identified payer is the federal government, no named enterprise customer appears in the customer graph (Customer graph), and insiders have sold $1.9MM with no buys (Form 4). Quarterly revenue of $2.8MM [16] is small against $7.5MM cash and roughly one quarter of runway (Reality check). If utility storage deployment or EV scrap volumes lag, the new capacity funded by grants could sit underutilized.

## Business today
ABTC sells lithium-ion battery recycling capacity and expects to recover battery-grade lithium, nickel, cobalt, and manganese [4]. It has constructed a first integrated recycling facility that takes waste and end-of-life batteries from the EV, stationary storage, and consumer electronics industries [11], using a process that covers multiple form factors and cathode chemistries [0]. Revenue was $4.3MM and cash was $7.5MM as of 2025-06-30 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
ABTC occupies the recycling node between deployed batteries and new cathode and cell production. Its process is positioned as a closed loop that returns recycled metals to cathode producers and cell makers [3][4]. Faster utility battery storage buildout increases both future retired packs and current manufacturing scrap, which is the feedstock that determines whether the fixed-cost facility can run at utilization [9].

## Non-linear case
If utility-scale storage buildout accelerates through 2028, the installed base generates a future stream of retired packs and near-term manufacturing scrap. ABTC's first facility is already ramping revenue, from $1.0MM in Q3 FY2025 to $2.8MM in Q4 FY2025 [16]. The $150MM DOE grant and $40.5MM tax credit are earmarked for a second, next-generation facility [14][17]. To become a multiple, ABTC must convert those awards into operating capacity, secure feedstock at economic prices [9], and complete the BASF-C4V demonstration of cathode-grade products [3]. Success would make recycling a domestic source of battery-grade material [4].

## Evidence
- ABTC has developed a universal lithium-ion battery recycling system for a wide range of form factors and cathode chemistries [0].
- ABTC and BASF continue to explore avenues of collaboration to accelerate commercialization [2].
- A contracted award covers commercial-scale development and demonstration of an integrated recycling system, production of cathode-grade metals, synthesis of cathode material by BASF, and cell fabrication and testing by C4V [3].
- ABTC expects to recover battery cathode grade lithium, nickel, cobalt, and manganese and will compete with recycling processors and primary producers [4].
- Success of recycling operations depends on securing adequate quantities of spent batteries and feedstock at economic prices [9].
- The first integrated recycling facility takes waste and end-of-life battery materials from EV, stationary storage, and consumer electronics industries [11].
- The Advanced Battery Consortium supports a $2MM project to accelerate development and demonstration at the integrated facility [12].
- A $19.5MM 48C tax credit supports the recycling facility in the Tahoe-Reno Industrial Center [13].
- An additional $40.5MM 48C tax credit supports a new, next-generation, commercial recycling facility [14].
- Fiscal fourth quarter 2025 revenue increased to $2.8MM from $1.0MM in the third quarter [16].
- The company was selected for, and successfully contracted, a $150MM DOE grant for an additional recycling facility [17].
