# ASYS: Utility-scale battery storage buildout accelerates through 2028, lifting silicon carbide wafer equipment demand

## Bear case first
ASYS has no measurable SiC revenue in the evidence; the filings describe SiC as an application, not a segment [0][1]. The customer evidence is thin: no named enterprise customer, and the only federal award is $0.9MM. There are no catalysts in the filings, no quantified management commitment to SiC, and no insider open-market activity. The same evidence supports a base case where ASYS is a diversified small-cap equipment vendor and the SiC theme remains a small part of a broad addressable market. If SiC wafer demand grows, ASYS may not capture pricing power without named design wins or qualification milestones.

## Business today
ASYS sells process equipment used in early-stage silicon and silicon carbide wafer manufacturing, then in semiconductor device manufacturing, advanced packaging, and electronics assembly [0]. Applications include silicon wafers for digital and analog devices, SiC wafers for power devices, optics, and ceramics [1]. Revenue was $79.4MM and cash was $17.9MM as of 2025-09-30 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
ASYS occupies the wafer-manufacturing equipment node, before semiconductor device manufacturing, packaging, and assembly [0]. It is an order 3 beneficiary of the utility storage buildout: inverter makers upgrade to SiC power modules, wafer suppliers expand capacity, and ASYS supplies a key input for making those wafers. The economic logic is that equipment orders arrive as capacity is committed, which can precede module shipments and end-market revenue.

## Non-linear case
If utility battery storage accelerates through 2028, inverter makers will qualify SiC power modules and wafer suppliers will add early-stage capacity. ASYS, as equipment maker at that first step, could see orders grow faster than its current revenue base if it wins a share of the capacity buildout [0]. What has to be true is that SiC becomes standard in large high-voltage inverters, wafer suppliers commit capex, and ASYS turns those commitments into purchase orders visible in filings. Operating leverage on a small revenue base would create the multiple. The low optionality score is a warning that this is an extension of the existing business, not a new revenue leg.

Share count rose 1.5 percent over 12 months and a shelf registration is on file, which are dilution risks.

## What would change our mind
The thesis would change if a 10-Q or 10-K reports SiC-specific revenue, named wafer customers, or equipment capacity orders. It would also change on Form 4 evidence of insider open-market buying or selling. A new USASpending award tied explicitly to silicon carbide wafer equipment would add government validation. Each event is observable in the corresponding filing and would test the mechanism.
