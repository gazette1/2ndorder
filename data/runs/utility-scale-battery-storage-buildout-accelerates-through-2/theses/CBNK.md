# CBNK: Bank with no disclosed storage exposure; the battery theme is off-map

## Bear case first
The short thesis fails at the first link: no filing ties CBNK to pumped hydro or energy storage [0]. The EIA text is a definitional footnote, not a business line. The company is protected by its business model: cash flows come from lending, not grid services, and the share count fell 2.2% in 12 months, indicating buybacks (SEC XBRL). The disconfirming data points are insider sales of $1,375M (Form 4) and a customer base that is mostly unnamed, but both are too thin to justify a short at a $608MM market cap.

## Business today
Revenue is not reported in the dossier (SEC XBRL). Cash is $34.7MM (SEC XBRL). The only filing content is an investor overview with an EIA electricity-sources table; it does not name a product, lending segment, or customer [0].

## Chain position
The mapped node is pumped hydro storage, order one, at risk as lithium-ion batteries enter frequency regulation and daily arbitrage. CBNK does not occupy that node in the evidence. The short economics only bind if the bank owns storage assets or lends to their operators, which no filing shows.

## Customer graph
Government: $3,013M in federal awards from the Department of the Treasury, in three awards of $700M, $700M, and $580M (USASpending). Enterprise: none named in the excerpts. Unproven: the bank's core customers are unnamed. Four other filers name CBNK (Customer graph), but the award sizes are small and may be program payments rather than lending.

## Insider and management signal
Open-market activity is net negative: two sales, zero buys, $1,375M of selling (Form 4). No management language appears in the filing; the EIA excerpt is not a management commitment [0]. Management conviction is unobservable from the evidence.

## Street view
No sell-side coverage is available: analyst count unknown, consensus n/a, mean target n/a. For a $608MM bank, that is thin coverage. A short has no institutional rating to trade against, and coverage initiation would itself be an event.

## Non-linear case
The downside compounds if the loan book contains storage or pumped-hydro credits that the excerpt omits. Falling battery costs and saturation of arbitrage and frequency-regulation revenue would pressure those borrowers, drive charge-offs, and consume the $34.7MM net cash cushion. The Treasury awards suggest a government-linked revenue niche that could shrink as energy transition funds move. A filing would need to reveal this exposure; none has.

## Evidence
- The investor overview reproduces an EIA definition that lists pumped storage and batteries among electricity sources [0].
- The same filing describes no CBNK storage activity, investment, revenue, or customer program [0].

## Sizing and survivability
Average daily dollar volume is $2,227M, about 15 trading days to build a position (Reality check). Net cash of $34.7MM and a falling share count put a floor under the stock, while the $608MM market cap limits short payoff. No shelf filing is noted in the dossier; the buyback reduces dilution risk and makes this a poor short candidate.

## What would change our mind
- A Form 4 with a cluster of insider open-market buys within one quarter would contradict the selling signal.
- A 10-Q or 8-K that discloses energy storage or pumped hydro loans would make the theme testable.
- A USASpending record or Treasury award that names CBNK as a storage-project lender would replace the unproven customer graph.
