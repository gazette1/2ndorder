# DUOT: Data center power demand creates a second revenue channel through mobile gas turbine fleet management.

## Bear case first
Why this name fails. A disconfirming reading is that the gas turbine work is an asset-management relationship, not a product sale with a visible backlog; the filing does not quantify fleet revenue or margins [4]. Insider open-market activity is zero over the trailing 12 months (Form 4). Customer evidence is thin: named counterparties are contract entities, and federal award dollars are $2.56M from one DOT contract (USASpending). The company has about 0.6 quarters of cash runway and a shelf on file, so dilution is a near-term risk (Reality check).

## Business today
The company designs, develops, deploys, and operates intelligent technology solutions, including edge data centers and power and energy consulting [0]. It sells hosting, colocation, and managed infrastructure services through edge data centers [7] and infrastructure-related services for data center deployments [14]. For the year ended 2025-12-31, revenue was $27.0M; cash at that date was $1.9M (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
DUOT occupies the on-site gas generation beneficiary node of the data center power adoption chain. The filing states the company has engaged with third parties for consulting and asset management for power solutions for large data centers supporting AI hyperscalers [3]. The economic logic is that data center operators facing grid interconnection delays may accept a premium for on-site generation; DUOT manages a mobile gas turbine fleet under contract [4].

## Customer graph
Fortress, New APR, and Sawgrass are named enterprise counterparties (Customer graph); Fortress paid a $5.0M cash advance recognized as revenue in 2025 [17]. The 10-Q refers to the fleet asset counterparty as New APR in one note [17] and Sawgrass in another [18] without explaining the relationship. The only government buyer is the Department of Transportation at $2.56M (USASpending). One other filer names the company (Customer graph). Customer evidence is thin; end-customer payments for the gas turbine fleet are not disclosed.

## Insider and management signal
No insider open-market buying or selling appeared in the trailing 12 months (Form 4). Management formed a subsidiary for infrastructure services [5] and signed the Fortress fleet contract [17], but the filing does not state quantified targets or committed capex. The signal is neutral: specific operations, no insider stake change.

## Street view
Sell-side coverage is absent: analyst count, consensus, and mean target are all unavailable (Street view). Thin coverage is part of the setup for an under-covered small cap, but it also means no external validation of the thesis.

## Non-linear case
If the Fortress fleet management contract expands from consulting and operations into a large managed fleet, revenue could scale beyond the current $27.0M base (SEC XBRL). The filing describes demand for power solutions for large data centers and AI hyperscalers [3] and DUOT provides management, sales, and operations for mobile gas turbines [4]. For that outcome, data center interconnection delays must persist, Fortress must deploy additional capital, and DUOT must convert this contract into a recurring asset-management business. The current filing evidence does not show that conversion.

## What would change our mind

- A Form 4 showing insider open-market purchases.
- A new federal award from DOE or DoD for on-site power or data center infrastructure (USASpending).
