# SMXT: Grid capex supercycle exposure is contractual, not a new testing franchise

## Bear case first
The 8-K's testing and commissioning clauses are obligations inside a single BESS EPC contract, not a standalone business line [12]. The thesis requires SMXT to sit at the testing/commissioning node, but the filing shows contractor testing, not third-party certification sold to utilities [16]. There are no insider buys, no named customers, no federal awards, and the share count rose 24.2 percent in 12 months (Form 4, USASpending, reality check). The filing does not quantify testing revenue or show a second project, leaving the revenue base behind the thesis an open question.

## Business today
SMXT acts as contractor under a utility-scale BESS agreement filed with its 8-K [0]. It procures, constructs, installs and tests the battery and all interconnection facilities required to connect to the local utility's transmission system [12]. Revenue was $90.98MM and cash was $7.97MM at 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
Testing and commissioning are contract deliverables inside the EPC scope; the contractor cannot energize the battery without the owner's written approval [7]. The owner holds the interconnection agreement [13][19]. SMXT sits at the EPC node, not the independent certification node, so the thesis's economic logic does not match the evidence.

## Customer graph
The buyer is unproven: the only counterparty is the "Owner" of the BESS project, not named in the excerpts [0]. No enterprise customer is named, federal awards are $0, and no other filer names SMXT (USASpending). Customer evidence is thin and unproven.

## Insider and management signal
No insider bought or sold stock in the trailing 12 months, a net $0 read on conviction (Form 4). Management language is contractually firm: $5M per day liquidated damages for missed mechanical completion, full risk for interconnection cost, and clear acceptance testing criteria [17][9][3].

## Street view
No sell-side coverage exists; analyst count is unknown, consensus is n/a, and mean target is n/a (Street view). For an under-covered $197MM small-cap, thin coverage is itself part of the setup.

## Non-linear case
For the thesis to pay a multiple, SMXT would need to convert this EPC execution into a repeatable testing/commissioning franchise with utilities as named customers. That path requires evidence outside this filing: follow-on awards, a separate testing/commissioning segment, or disclosed backlog. Any pipeline beyond this project is an open question.

## What would change our mind
An open-market insider purchase on a Form 4 would signal conviction absent today. A second named customer or a testing/commissioning-only award in an 8-K or 10-K would confirm the grid capex revenue path. A federal award in USASpending would establish a government buyer.
