# SMXT: BESS EPC contractor in the grid capex chain

## Bear case first
SolarMax's filings describe BESS EPC, not transmission or substation work [5][8]. That places it in a competitive storage segment, not the scarce transmission labor pool. The dossier shows no insider open-market buying (Form 4), a 24.2% share count increase in 12 months (Reality check), and revenue concentrated in one customer contract [7]. Customer evidence is limited to four enterprise LLCs, with $0 federal awards (USASpending). The filings do not show financial targets or a transmission backlog.

## Business today
SolarMax performs EPC services for commercial BESS systems and states this has been its primary business since Q3 2025 [5]. EPC revenue from the Longfellow contract was $60.2MM, 66.1% of revenue [7]. Total revenue was $90.982MM, with cash of $7.967MM (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
Mapped position: transmission and substation engineering and construction contractor, order 1 beneficiary, near horizon. SolarMax's named work is BESS EPC, not transmission or substation construction [5][8]. The economic logic is that EPC contractors capture project value through labor and equipment scarcity.

## Customer graph
Named buyers are Longfellow BESS I, LLC, Naguabo BESS, LLC, Yabucoa BESS, LLC, and Navboot Holdco, LLC; all are enterprise LLCs (customer graph). The other three contracts were dated December 31, 2025 [8]. Federal awards are $0 (USASpending), and 0 other filers name the company (customer graph). Customer evidence is thin: the Longfellow contract produced 66.1% of revenue [7], and no revenue from the later contracts is disclosed.

## Insider and management signal
Trailing 12-month Form 4 data show no open-market insider buying or selling (Form 4). Management describes services commenced in Q3 2025 [0] and a specific 430 MWh Texas project [6]. The filings do not state quantified financial targets.

## Street view
Sell-side aggregators show no coverage: analyst count unknown, consensus n/a, mean target n/a (Street view). No firm rating actions are visible. For an under-covered small-cap, thin coverage is part of the setup.

## Non-linear case
Completion of the Longfellow 430 MWh project [6] and the three December 31 BESS agreements [8] would scale revenue from the 2025 base of $90.982MM (SEC XBRL). The 10-K projects a U.S. solar market over $125,000MM by 2030 [4]. The path requires the four LLC counterparties to fund and finish projects and SolarMax to win follow-on work. A multiple depends on recurring EPC revenue and data-center-driven grid demand.

## Evidence
- EPC services on the first large-scale storage initiative commenced in Q3 2025 [0].
- The U.S. solar market is expected to be valued at over $125,000MM by 2030 [4].
- Since Q3 2025, the primary business has been negotiating contracts and performing EPC services for solar-based BESS commercial systems [5].
- EPC services had commenced on a 430 MWh battery storage project in Texas under an agreement with Longfellow dated July 31, 2025 [6].
- EPC services on the Longfellow contract generated revenue of $60.2MM, or 66.1% of revenue [7].
- On December 31, 2025, SolarMax entered three EPC agreements for large-scale BESS systems, two in Puerto Rico and one in Corpus Christi, Texas [8].

## What would change our mind
An 8-K or 10-K disclosing a transmission or substation EPC contract would shift the thesis from BESS-only to direct grid capex exposure. A Form 4 showing open-market insider buying would strengthen conviction; an open-market sale would weaken it. A federal award in USASpending naming SolarMax would broaden the customer base beyond the four named enterprise LLCs.
