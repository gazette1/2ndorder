# NEXT: Grid capex supercycle thesis fails because NextDecade is an LNG project owner, not a grid EPC contractor

## Bear case first
The strongest disconfirming reading is that NextDecade is an LNG development company whose filing evidence consists entirely of change orders on the Rio Grande liquefaction EPC agreement with Bechtel, with no transmission, substation, or data center load work anywhere in the record [0][1][4][7]. Revenue is $0 and net cash is -$8,683.6MM (SEC XBRL), so the company may need capital before the 2026-2030 cycle begins. Insider buying is positive, but the filings do not show any customer or contract tied to grid investment. The grid capex theme does not appear in the company's own disclosures, which is the central failure of the thesis.

## Business today
NextDecade, through Rio Grande LNG, LLC, is the owner under an Amended and Restated Fixed Price Turnkey EPC Agreement with Bechtel Energy Inc. for Trains 1 and 2 of the Rio Grande Natural Gas Liquefaction Facility [0]. Revenue is $0 and cash is $143.782MM (SEC XBRL). The change orders cover equipment procurement, design revisions, commodity index adjustments, and tariff reimbursement [1][2][3][5][6].

## Chain position
The seed map places transmission and substation EPC contractors as order-1 beneficiaries of grid capex. NextDecade does not occupy that node; it is the owner that pays the EPC contractor, so a dollar of utility capex flows to Bechtel-type contractors, not to NextDecade. The economic logic of the chain does not connect to the company's existing business.

## Customer graph
Unproven. The excerpts name no paying customer, federal awards are $0 (USASpending), and 5 other filers name the company with the relationship unspecified. Bechtel is a contractor paid by NextDecade, not a customer. LNG off-takers are not identified in the evidence.

## Insider and management signal
Form 4 shows net open-market buying of $6.955MM across 5 buys and 0 sales by 4 insiders, a positive conviction signal. Management language in the filings is administrative, limited to change order execution and pricing adjustments [1][2][3][5]. No management discussion of grid, transmission, or data centers appears in the evidence.

## Street view
Sell-side coverage is absent from the evidence: no analyst count, consensus, or mean target is reported. Thin coverage is typically a setup for a small-cap, but here it matches a company with $0 revenue (SEC XBRL) and a construction-stage project [0]. No rating actions are available.

## Non-linear case
A multiple of today's business would require the grid supercycle to create a new revenue stream, through an acquisition, joint venture, or grid-related contract award. None of those paths appears in the filing evidence. The LNG project itself offers a large outcome, but it is tied to gas export volumes and FID milestones, not data center load. Without a new business segment, the theme cannot be the source of upside.

## Sizing and survivability
A small fund can build a position in about 12 trading days at $20.494MM average daily dollar volume, so liquidity is adequate. Survivability threatens the thesis: net cash is -$8,683.6MM, cash runway is about 3.4 quarters, and share count grew 2 percent in 12 months (SEC XBRL). The company may not reach the 2026-2030 grid cycle without LNG project financing.

## What would change our mind
- A 10-K or 10-Q MD&A, or an earnings call transcript, in which management discusses transmission, substations, or data center load as a business opportunity.
- An 8-K or Form 4 showing a grid-related contract award, a named utility or data center customer, or insider buying tied to a grid program.
- A federal award (USASpending) or contract disclosure naming a utility, transmission developer, or data center operator as a paying customer.
