# CEPL: On-site generation for AI-driven grid capex

## Bear case first
The filings describe the AI data center theme [4] and a reference design [13], but no design wins, no named data center order, and no quantified market size. Revenue is concentrated in two customers [2], and the only dated event is a CARB Notice of Violation [3]. Insider buying is small (Form 4), and the customer graph is thin: no federal awards and two named entities (USASpending; customer graph).

## Business today
Capstone sells distributed power generation and microgrid systems, including CHP, ICHP, CCHP, and inverter-based island-capable equipment [0][1], for data center, station power, port, and critical power applications [5]. Reported revenue was $106.004MM and cash was $28.179MM as of 2026-03-31 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
Order-2 beneficiary in the on-site generation and microgrid developer node, mid horizon. Utility interconnection costs and reliability requirements make self-supply economic [6][8]. Its inverter-based technologies are the stabilizing backbone for microgrids [1].

## Customer graph
Named customers are Horizon Power Systems and E-Finity, both enterprise entities [2]; E-Finity is identified as a distributor [14]. Federal awards are $0 (USASpending), and zero other filers name the company (customer graph). Data center end users are unproven.

## Insider and management signal
Form 4 shows net open-market buying of $295M from 6 buys and 0 sales by 6 insiders (Form 4). Management says demand is accelerating due to AI and data centers [4] and that it has begun using its microgrid solutions in an AI/data center reference design [13]. It also discloses AI-infrastructure investments [17], with no quantified targets.

## Street view
Unknown analyst count, consensus n/a, mean target n/a (street view). No firm rating actions are available. Thin coverage is part of the setup.

## Non-linear case
The reference design [13] becomes a standard for AI data center or critical power projects and scales through the two customers [2][14]. Revenue would multiply if the company supplies integrated microgrids and aftermarket services, not just components. That requires slow grid interconnection, volatile demand charges, and no lasting CARB NOV [3] impact. A single design win would be material to the small revenue base, but none is named.

## Evidence
- Distributed generation and energy distribution solutions, including CHP, ICHP, CCHP [0].
- Inverter-based technologies run grid-parallel or island mode and support microgrids with renewables and storage [1].
- Horizon Power Systems and E-Finity were 41% and 12% of quarterly revenue [2].
- CARB issued a Notice of Violation over 27 distributed generation units [3].
- Demand for on-site generation is accelerating, driven by AI infrastructure and data centers [4].
- Offerings cover data centers, station power, and ports [5].
- Grid power is usually less costly than simple-cycle distributed generation, but utilities may impose interconnection costs and fees [6].
- On-site generation helps when interconnection is costly, reliability is critical, or peak shaving is advantageous [8].
- The company has begun using its microgrid solutions in an AI and data center reference design package [13].
- E-Finity is one of the company's distributors [14].
- It has made investments and may continue to make partnerships related to AI-infrastructure and data centers [17].

## Sizing and survivability
At $3.379MM average daily volume, about 10 trading days to build a position (reality check). Net cash of $2.859MM and 44.6 quarters of runway cover near-term needs (reality check). Share count up 1.1% with shelf on file means dilution is a watch item, not an immediate threat.

## What would change our mind
A named data center customer would appear in a 10-Q or 10-K and would validate the reference design [13]. A Form 4 showing insider sales or no follow-through buying would weaken the signal. A federal award would appear in USASpending and broaden the customer graph.
