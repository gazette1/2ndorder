# GWRS: Data center power cost risk is real, but regulation and insider buying make this an avoid, not a short

## Bear case first

A short fails because ACC regulation sets revenue requirements from rate base, allowed return, and test-year operating expenses, so a power cost increase is recoverable in a future rate case, not an automatic margin kill [8][10][11]. Recovery is not immediate, and management calls power costs beyond control [6], so a long is weak too. The same mechanism caps upside if power costs fall [11]. Filings show no power cost adjustment clause, no electricity cost share, and no data center customer. The disconfirming read is thin customer evidence, not insider selling, because Form 4 shows net buying.

## Business today

GWRS sells ACC-regulated water and wastewater service with fixed and volumetric fees [11]. Revenue rose on ICFA, the Tucson acquisition, and organic growth, offset by depreciation and interest [0]. Revenue was $55,758M, cash $4,080M at 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position

Map node: large industrial electricity consumer. GWRS uses electricity for meter reading and data transmission [4], so data center-driven power and grid charge increases hit operating expense before rates reset [6][11].

## Customer graph

Government: $125M in Department of the Interior awards (USASpending). Enterprise: no named customers (customer graph). Unproven: retail ratepayers, since filings show only aggregate connections [7]. One other filer names GWRS (customer graph).

## Insider and management signal

Form 4: net $7,090M in 9 buys, 0 sales by 5 insiders. Management is cautious: power costs are beyond control [6], growth depends on rate cases and connections [3]. Insider buying is the only direct positive signal.

## Street view

No analyst count, consensus, or target is available from sell-side aggregators (Street view). This is an under-covered small-cap. Average daily dollar volume is $932,654 (Reality check).

## Non-linear case

The short's nonlinear path is a large decline if power prices spike while GWRS is between rate cases. Rates use a backward-looking revenue requirement, so margins compress until the ACC resets [11]. Accelerated capex would raise depreciation and interest before new rates, the offset in the 2026 release [2]. A sustained power shock plus capital program could turn the utility into a cash burn, with the shelf as dilution.

## Evidence

- Revenue increase from ICFA, Tucson acquisition, organic growth; offset by depreciation and interest [0][2].
- Strategy: more connections, operating efficiencies, ACC-approved rates [3].
- Meters transmit readings via tower and cellular units [4].
- Power costs are beyond control and can increase unpredictably [6].
- Tucson acquisition added about 2,200 connections and $7,700M rate base [7].
- ACC revenue requirement from rate base, allowed return, and operating expenses; fixed and volumetric fees [8][10][11].

## Sizing and survivability

Small funds can own modest positions, but 36 days to build at $932,654 daily volume limits tactical entry (Reality check). Cash $4,080M, negative net cash $-129,618M (Reality check). Share count up 4.9% and shelf filed are dilution risks (Reality check).

## What would change our mind

A 10-K or 8-K quantifying electricity cost share or adding a power cost recovery clause would weaken the short. A Form 4 insider sale would remove the positive signal. A federal award or named customer tied to data center water service would move GWRS to the revenue node; USASpending or an 8-K would show it.
