# ONDS: Water rationing makes water-utility operational technology security a budget line

## Bear case first
At the stated $3.7B market cap, the current price embeds a large future for a company with $50.7M revenue (SEC XBRL). The filings describe critical infrastructure protection generally, but never name water utilities, networked meters, or remote-control valves. Customer evidence is thin: no named customers, no federal awards (USASpending), and no other filers name Ondas. Form 4 insiders sold $44.2M net over the trailing 12 months (Form 4). The order-2 water thesis requires a segment shift the evidence does not yet show.

Deployed capabilities include Counter-UAS, force protection, border security, and critical infrastructure protection [1]. Revenue was $50.7M and cash was $550.7M at 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
This thesis places Ondas as an order-2 beneficiary of water rationing. New networked meters and remote-control valves expand the attack surface, utilities buy industrial control system security, and water utilities become essential targets. The economic logic depends on Ondas converting its existing critical infrastructure protection position into water-specific ICS security, which the filings do not mention.

## Non-linear case
If water becomes premium-priced and family purchase limits are enforced, utilities face public consequences from meter or treatment outages. Security budgets could rise with regulatory scrutiny, and Ondas's critical infrastructure protection platforms could become the entry point for recurring monitoring and service contracts. A single named water utility or a federal water-ICS security award would be the first proof point. Until that appears, the non-linear case is an open question.
