# AIRJ: Water rationing makes independent water supply cost-justified for households and small businesses, expanding an early-stage atmospheric water generation installed base

## Bear case first
The same evidence reads as a pre-commercial business. Revenue is not reported, cash is $59,167 (SEC XBRL), and the 10-K describes a technology that "at commercial scale will mitigate water scarcity," not current sales [0]. No insider has bought or sold stock in the trailing 12 months (Form 4), and no named customer or federal award appears in the dossier (USASpending). The filings do not quantify the cost advantage over prior methods or give a target price for harvested water. A shelf registration and 21.4% share count growth in 12 months point to dilution as the near-term financing mechanism.

## Business today
AirJoule is developing an atmospheric water generation platform that produces pure distilled water from air for businesses and consumers [0]. It completed field deployments and demonstrations in 2025 and has strategic partners [4]. Revenue was not reported and cash was $59,167 as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
The thesis places AirJoule at the equipment supplier node of the alternative water supply and reuse chain, an order 1 beneficiary with a mid-horizon payoff. As rationing makes municipal water unreliable, households and small businesses face higher effective cost for essential and discretionary use, making independent supply cost-justified. That dynamic expands a small installed base for equipment suppliers before conventional utilities see revenue shifts.

## Non-linear case
Potable certification for municipal and industrial applications would open a customer segment beyond the consumer and business markets [1]. The UN data in the filing puts four billion people in severe water scarcity for at least one month per year [2], and the filing says on-site generation can reduce reliance on municipal systems strained by data centers and advanced manufacturing [7]. If the AirJoule cost of water beats the failed energy-intensive methods [3] and the company reaches commercial scale, the revenue base could be a multiple of today's zero. What has to be true: TCEQ approval, a repeatable installation, and a disclosed unit price below alternatives.

## Evidence
- [0] The platform produces pure distilled water from air and, at commercial scale, will mitigate water scarcity through distributed generation for businesses and consumers.
- [1] In 2025, the company began discussions with TCEQ around potable water certification for municipal and industrial applications.
- [2] The UN data cited in the filing estimates nearly three-quarters of the world population is water-insecure, with four billion people in severe scarcity at least one month per year.
- [3] Prior atmospheric water harvesting methods failed to achieve competitive cost because of energy requirements.
- [4] The company completed field deployments and demonstrations in 2025 with strategic partners.
- [5] The company holds independent IP for heating, cooling, and low-cost harvesting of potable water from air.
- [7] On-site AirJoule generation can reduce reliance on municipal water systems strained by data centers and advanced manufacturing.
