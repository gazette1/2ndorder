# TrueBlue, Inc. (TBI): Water meter-to-cash is not visible in the filings and the company's cash position is the binding constraint

## Bear case first
Filings mention software-as-a-service only as an accounting item: amortization in SG&A and capitalized internal development costs [0][4]. Adjusted EBITDA and adjusted SG&A both exclude this amortization [1][2]. No excerpt describes meter-to-cash, water metering, or third-party SaaS revenue. The company lost $19.8M in the 13 weeks ended March 29, 2026, versus $14.3M a year earlier, and adjusted EBITDA was negative [3]. Insider buying is small and offset by a sale (Form 4), and the known government awards are Department of Transportation work, not water utility contracts (USASpending).

## Business today
The 8-K excerpts show revenue, net loss, and adjusted EBITDA, but they do not describe the core product or customer [3]. The only software references are amortization of software-as-a-service assets and expected capitalized development costs [0][4]. Cash was $24.5M as of 2025-12-28 (SEC XBRL); the dossier facts do not include a revenue figure.

## Chain position
The seed thesis puts TBI at the meter-to-cash SaaS node, with utilities replacing legacy billing and paying per endpoint. The evidence puts TBI on the cost side, as an operator of internally developed software assets, not a vendor selling those assets [4]. No revenue per endpoint or third-party contract appears in the filings.

## Customer graph
No named enterprise customer appears in the excerpts. USASpending shows $18.2M in federal awards, all from the Department of Transportation, including $4.3M, $3.0M, and $1.6M awards (USASpending). Three other filers name TBI. These are government buyers, not water utilities. Customer evidence for the meter-to-cash theme is unproven.

## Insider and management signal
Trailing 12-month open-market insider activity is net $134.4K bought across 3 buys and 1 sale by 3 insiders (Form 4). That is modest. Management language in the filings addresses SaaS assets only as amortization and capitalized costs, with no named program or target tied to metering [0][4].

## Street view
No analyst count, consensus, or mean target is available from the dossier; sell-side reports are paywalled and unlinked. For this name, thin coverage is not a hidden setup because the company has negative adjusted EBITDA and a short cash runway.

## Non-linear case
For TBI to become a multiple of today's value, its internal SaaS development would have to become a commercial meter-to-cash product with per-endpoint contracts. That would require product launches, customer wins, and regulatory water cap enforcement. The excerpts provide no basis for those steps. A more plausible non-linear path is a return to positive adjusted EBITDA and a deleveraging event, but the filing evidence does not establish that path either.

## Evidence
- Amortization of software as a service assets is reported in selling, general and administrative expense [0].
- Adjusted EBITDA excludes amortization of software as a service assets, acquisition integration costs, and other items [1].
- Adjusted SG&A excludes amortization of software as a service assets [2].
- Q1 2026 net loss was $19,795K versus $14,348K in Q1 2025; adjusted EBITDA was -$3,050K versus -$3,922K; SaaS asset amortization was $1,259K versus $1,093K [3].
- Fiscal 2026 capex and capitalized costs for SaaS asset development are expected to be $13M-$17M, with about $1M for SaaS assets [4].

## Sizing and survivability
Average daily dollar volume is $2,592,452, so a position would take about 95 trading days to build (Reality check). Net cash is -$12.6M and cash runway is about 1.7 quarters (Reality check). Share count rose 1.9% in 12 months (Reality check), and these facts make survival the first question.

## What would change our mind
- A 10-Q or 10-K that reports SaaS or meter-to-cash as a revenue line, not as an internal expense component.
- A Form 4 showing a meaningful increase in open-market insider buying above the trailing net $134.4K run rate.
- A new federal award in USASpending from a water utility or water agency rather than the Department of Transportation.
