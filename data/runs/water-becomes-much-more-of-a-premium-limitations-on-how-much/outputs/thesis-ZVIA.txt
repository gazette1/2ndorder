# ZVIA: Avoid a third-party bottler with no water-scarcity exposure, no catalysts, and a net insider sale

## Bear case first
The short case is that ZVIA sits at the brewing, bottling, and canning node with no water-scarcity linkage in its filings [0][1], no catalysts, and a $528.8K net insider sale (Form 4). What protects the company is the balance sheet: $25.4M cash funds roughly 21.6 quarters of operation (Reality check), and $161.3M revenue (SEC XBRL) gives the short a large base to overcome. Third-party production [0][1] leaves ZVIA without owned water rights, but also without fixed-asset impairment risk. The filings name no customers, so the short cannot point to concentration risk; the evidence gap cuts both ways.

## Business today
The filing evidence describes ZVIA only as a company whose products are manufactured and maintained at third-party beverage production and warehousing facilities in the U.S. and Canada [0][1]. Revenue is $161.3M and cash is $25.4M (SEC XBRL). The specific products and end buyers are not identified in the excerpts.

## Chain position
ZVIA occupies the brewing, bottling, and beverage canning node, order 2, at risk, mid horizon. Beverage production requires process and ingredient water with limited substitution, so municipal supply tightening raises input costs before prices can adjust. As a third-party producer [0][1], ZVIA does not own springs or long-term water rights, which places it in the margin-compression bucket of the water-scarcity theme.

## Customer graph

## Insider and management signal
Trailing 12-month Form 4 data show one open-market sale of $528.8K and zero buys (Form 4). Management language is procedural, describing third-party production locations [0][1], with no mention of water strategy, committed capital, or quantified targets. The absence of insider buying and the absence of thematic commitment read as low conviction.

## Street view

## Non-linear case
The downside path compounds if municipal water rationing arrives faster than ZVIA can pass through costs. As a third-party producer [0][1], ZVIA would face higher process and ingredient water costs while competitors with owned water rights gain a pricing advantage. Margins compress on the $161.3M revenue base (SEC XBRL), forcing a re-rating from going concern to capacity-constrained bottler. That outcome requires water costs to rise enough to break the third-party manufacturing model.

## What would change our mind
A Form 4 showing open-market insider buying would contradict the current selling signal. A 10-Q or 8-K naming a water-scarcity program, owned water rights, or a premium water product would add optionality to a theme absent from the filing language. A federal award to ZVIA (USASpending) would add the first government buyer and break the unproven customer label.
