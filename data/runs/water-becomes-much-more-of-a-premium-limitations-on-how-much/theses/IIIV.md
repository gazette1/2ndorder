# IIIV: Monthly water purchase caps force utilities onto configurable cloud billing platforms

## Bear case first
The April 2025 acquisition is small: total net assets acquired were $10.3MM, and $5.1MM of that is goodwill [1]. The filings describe the deal only as expanding existing public sector utility billing offerings, with no mention of water rationing, dynamic tariffs, or usage alerts [0][2]. No named customers, federal awards, or implementation milestones appear in the excerpts (USASpending). One insider sold $99,001 net over twelve months (Form 4). The disconfirming read is that this was a routine tuck-in with no strategic pivot.

## Business today
The company sells vertical-market payment software and services, including public sector utility billing software. Six-month revenue to March 31, 2026 was $112.3MM with net income from continuing operations of $3.7MM [0]. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $213.2MM and cash was $66.7MM (SEC XBRL).

## Chain position
IIIV occupies the utility billing and meter data management node, one step after meter hardware and one step before rate enforcement. AMI deployments generate interval usage data that legacy billing systems cannot price under tiered rates or monthly caps, which creates recurring demand for configurable cloud platforms and multi-year implementation contracts.

## Customer graph
No named enterprise or government customer appears in the excerpts. Federal award dollars are $0 (USASpending), and four other filers name the company in their own filings. The only quantified customer asset is $10.3MM of net assets from the acquired utility billing business [1]. Customer evidence is thin, and no agency or utility is identified as a payer.

## Insider and management signal
One insider recorded one open-market buy and seven sales over the trailing twelve months, for net open-market proceeds of $99,001 (Form 4). Management language is general: the acquisition was made to "expand the Company's public sector utility billing software offerings" [0][2], with no quantified targets or explicit commitment to the water theme.

## Street view
Sell-side aggregators report no analyst count, no consensus, and no mean target; the reports are paywalled. Thin coverage is itself part of the setup for an under-covered small-cap, but it also means no external validation of the thesis.

## Non-linear case
If water rationing becomes standard, utilities must enforce monthly purchase limits, tiered pricing, and real-time cap notifications that legacy billing suites cannot support. The acquired platform could win multi-year implementation contracts that make revenue a multiple of the $10.3MM asset base [1]. For that path, the platform must be configurable for dynamic tariffs and usage alerts, which the filings do not confirm. The non-linear outcome depends on regulation forcing a replacement cycle.

## Sizing and survivability
Average daily dollar volume is $8.4MM, so a small fund can build a position in about four trading days (Reality check). Cash of $66.7MM (SEC XBRL) provides runway, and no dilution event or shelf appears in the excerpts. The acquisition is small, so the thesis depends on future contracts rather than current scale.
