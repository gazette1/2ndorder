## Non-linear case
The compounding downside path requires municipal water rationing or pricing to bind before Zevia can raise shelf prices. Third-party bottlers would then allocate capacity to their own or higher-margin water contracts, squeezing Zevia's output. Input costs rise, gross margin contracts, and the shelf registration becomes a dilution vehicle if cash burn accelerates. Because no customers are named, a key account loss would not show up until the next quarterly filing. The non-linear move is down toward net cash and operating distress, not a valuation multiple expansion.
