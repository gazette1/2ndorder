# SWIM: Water rationing turns the fiberglass conversion story into a short

## Bear case first
The short thesis is that drought-driven water restrictions cut pool installations [4]. The protection for the company is material conversion: fiberglass pool starts are outpacing the broader in-ground market, and demand is supported by lower upfront and lifecycle costs, faster installation, and fewer chemicals [0][1][3]. Even under rationing, fiberglass could take share from concrete. The disconfirming read of the same evidence: insider buying is only $315.7K on a $629M market cap (Form 4), customer evidence is $3.0M of federal awards with zero named enterprise customers (USASpending), and the filing risk factor is generic with no quantified exposure [4].

## Business today
Latham sells fiberglass in-ground pools and complementary products to homeowners, with revenue of $545.9M and cash of $71.0M as of 2025-12-31 (SEC XBRL). The portfolio is highly complementary and covers a wide range of residential solutions [2].

## Chain position
SWIM sits at order 3, pool construction and maintenance services. Fewer filled pools cut both new construction backlogs and recurring maintenance visits, because service routes depend on minimum visit frequency for chemical balancing and equipment checks.

## Customer graph
Enterprise: unproven, zero named customers in the excerpts. Government: $3.0M in federal awards, including $1.0M and $0.8M from the Department of Agriculture and $426.8K from the Department of Homeland Security (USASpending). Sixteen other filers name the company. Customer evidence is thin for a $545.9M revenue base.

## Insider and management signal
Two insiders bought $315.7K net on the open market with zero sales over the trailing 12 months (Form 4). The buying is directionally positive but small against the $629M market cap. Management language is generic: drought and water management initiatives could result in decreased pool installations and negatively impact sales [4], with no named initiatives or capital commitments.

## Street view
Coverage is effectively absent: unknown analyst count, consensus n/a, mean target n/a. Thin coverage means no sell-side has framed the water-rationing risk, which is part of the setup.

## Non-linear case
The downside compounds if sustained drought triggers municipal rationing ordinances. New permits are delayed or denied, existing pools are left unfilled, and maintenance routes lose the minimum visit frequency required for chemical balancing. Backlogs shrink while fixed costs remain, cash burns through the $71.0M balance, and the -$208.8M net cash position (SEC XBRL) forces dilutive financing. Revenue falls from the $545.9M base without a matching drop in operating costs.

## Evidence
- Fiberglass conversion driven by homeowner awareness of benefits [0]
- Fiberglass pool starts outpacing the broader in-ground market [1]
- Residential pool portfolio is highly complementary [2]
- Demand driven by material conversion, lower costs, faster installation, fewer chemicals [3]
- Drought or water management initiatives could decrease pool installations [4]

## Sizing and survivability
Average daily dollar volume is $5.2M, about 48 trading days to build a position. Net cash is -$208.8M with $71.0M cash on hand, and the share count is up 1.1 percent in 12 months (SEC XBRL). The negative net cash and existing dilution make the equity fragile if the downside path plays out.

## What would change our mind
- A Form 4 open-market insider sale would reverse the current buy signal.
- A 10-Q or 10-K naming a water-restriction program or quantified revenue impact would make the risk measurable.
- A federal award above the $3.0M base (USASpending) would be the first named customer validation.
