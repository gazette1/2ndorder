# TBI: A water meter-to-cash SaaS beneficiary in name only

## Bear case first
The disconfirming read is that the filings describe a labor-services company with internal software costs, not a software vendor; the adjusted EBITDA lines identify hiring tax credits and workforce reduction costs [3]. Every SaaS mention in the filings is an accounting adjustment to SG&A or a capitalized internal development cost [0][1][2][4], not a revenue line. The theme needs a meter-to-cash platform, water utilities, and per-endpoint recurring revenue; the filings contain none. Customer evidence is transportation awards (USASpending), not water. Net cash is negative, so there is no evident capital to fund a new business.

## Business today
The provided filing excerpts do not name a product line. The 8-K presents margin percentages of revenue without a revenue dollar amount in the supplied excerpt [3]; cash was $24.51MM (SEC XBRL). The 10-K guides to $13MM to $17MM of fiscal 2026 capital expenditures and capitalized costs for development of software-as-a-service assets, about $1MM for SaaS assets [4].

## Chain position
Assigned position is order 3, beneficiary, mid horizon in the meter-to-cash SaaS adoption chain. The logic is recurring subscription revenue per endpoint as utilities replace legacy billing. Filings show no SaaS revenue and no water metering product.

## Customer graph

Three other filers name TBI (USASpending). For the water theme, customers are unproven: no water utility, meter vendor, or SaaS buyer is shown.

## Street view

## Non-linear case
The upside path would require converting TBI into a meter-to-cash SaaS vendor with recurring per-endpoint revenue from water utilities. That would demand a new product, new customers, and capital. The current cash runway of about 1.7 quarters and negative net cash (reality check) narrow that path. A small acquisition remains possible, but no filing signals one. The multiple depends on evidence that does not yet exist.

## What would change our mind
A 10-K or 8-K reporting a meter-to-cash SaaS product, a named water utility customer, or SaaS subscription revenue. A federal award from a water-related agency, such as the EPA or Bureau of Reclamation, in USASpending. A Form 4 showing large open-market insider buying after a disclosed SaaS pivot.
